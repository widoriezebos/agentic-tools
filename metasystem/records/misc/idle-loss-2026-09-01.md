Wido's question, verbatim: "what mechanism broke down. You must claim a
goal and then work on slices of that goal that are 4 hours max. Every
slice needs to land at remote; and if that exceeds the budget of the
slice by 50% we have a role (steward?) that needs to intervene and act
accordingly. That did not happen. There is at least one thing broken."

## What worked

Slices landed at remote all night (six design revisions). The 4-hour
budget fence held. The breach-stop intervened lawfully at the fence.
The job cap kept the dead worker from lingering as a husk. Gap-stops
kept invented bytes out of the tree.

## What broke, ranked

1. FAILED WORKER, NO DELIVERY (machinery): the reaper marked the job
   failed within seconds and no mechanism carried that fact to the
   delegator or the human. The alert channel design's enumerated
   classes did NOT include "delegate job failed under a claimed goal"
   — the very event of this incident was missing from the feature
   being built to fix it. Fixed the same morning: the class enters the
   design by Wido's word (see the goal's next step and the revision-7
   fold brief).
2. INTERVENTION WITHOUT SUCCESSION (machinery): the breach-stop stops
   and records, but does not alert (channel absent — covered by the
   stop-awaiting-resume class once live) and does not offer the work
   back to anyone. Succession for delegator-dispatched jobs exists
   nowhere; the steward revives only its own continuations. Candidate
   home: the alert channel's producers plus a later steward slice;
   raised in the morning report rather than silently goaled — scope is
   Wido's.
3. WRONG-CONDITION WAIT (seat conduct, m3): the second specimen in two
   days. Remedy tokened as goal:delegate-wait-primitive — one honest
   wait-for-job tool that watches the job record, always terminates,
   and always prints status and failure reason. Until it lands, the
   conduct rule: wait on job records, never artifacts; every wait
   bounded; every wait ends with a status check.
