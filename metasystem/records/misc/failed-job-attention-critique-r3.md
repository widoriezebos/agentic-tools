## FJA-R3-RAISE-REPLAY-GC-LOSS — high, material=True

CLAIM: A lawful garbage-collection interleaving can still erase the only raise-replay key before narration, reproducing silent loss. The design says, “evidence GC cannot do it: its 5,400-second grace window is at least eight default ticks” (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:563-566). That assumes those ticks occur. A sweep can durably create the episode and queued notification, the tick process can die before NarrateDigest, the goal can then be released or superseded, and the independently invoked collector can prune the terminal record after 5,400 seconds. The next tick has no failed-job candidate, so it neither opens the retained episode nor narrates its raise; its withdrawal phase also removes the queued notification because the record no longer stands. Fixture 12 immediately runs the recovery tick after flipping chainClosed and therefore does not replay this lawful outage-plus-collection sequence. An implementer needs either a durable replay anchor independent of the job record, retention through successful narration, or an explicitly narrowed visibility contract and corresponding fixture.

EVIDENCE: The design restricts candidate episode opens to terminal records still present in artifacts/agents/jobs and says retained history without a standing record is never opened (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:428-454). Its loss proof relies on the quoted grace-period assertion (lines 553-567). Garbage collection returns false from keepsSpendingFact once the bound goal is not the current claimed revision and then removes a mirrored terminal record after the grace period (metasystem/internal/evidence/gc.go:375-449,477-511). The supervision Stop hook invokes that collector independently of steward ticks (metasystem/scripts/agents/supervision-hook.sh:265).

## FJA-R3-PENDING-AT-MOST-ONCE-CRASH — high, material=True

CLAIM: The arbitration lock does not establish the design's claimed at-most-once delivery boundary across a post-send crash or local cleanup failure. Revision 3 says, “one queued file is delivered at most ONCE per queueing, because the send and the removal are one critical section” (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:509-510). The external send happens before markIntentNotified and MarkDelivered. If the process dies, the intent scan fails, or removal fails after the notifier exits successfully, the flock is released while the pending file remains; an overlapping pass then re-stats an existing file and sends again. For chain closure, goal release, or record removal—the off-switches delivery deliberately does not re-check—this can also exceed the claimed single stale delivery. Fixture 3b case C proves only the success-and-removal path and cannot establish the stated crash bound.

EVIDENCE: The shipped ordering is Deliver, then markIntentNotified, then MarkDelivered (metasystem/internal/steward/notify.go:83-96). Its existing comment already acknowledges that a crash between the post-send writes repeats delivery (lines 86-89). The proposed flock serializes those operations against another pass but cannot atomically commit an external side effect with os.Remove. Fixture 3b describes no injected death or post-send cleanup failure (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:616-631).

## FJA-R3-CHANNEL-MIGRATION-STALE-RUNNER — high, material=True

CLAIM: The channel-migration end condition is not stable against an already-running failed-job-attention runner. The design declares “ZERO private nag deliveries after that first post-landing tick” (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:218-224), but migration only removes SweepWorkEscalations from the newly landed RunTick and marks private episodes Cleared. A resident old binary can acquire arbitration after that tick, observe the still-failed record and existing private episode, and queue another private nag because the nag predicate checks Standing and Acknowledged but not Cleared. Its delivery predicate likewise skips only absent or Acknowledged episodes, so it can send after migration. The goal-facing residual specifies cleanup but neither runner quiescence nor Cleared as a private-delivery off-switch, and section 6 has no migration-overlap fixture. The owner is now named, but the promised end is therefore not proved.

EVIDENCE: The private nag set excludes acknowledged episodes but does not exclude cleared episodes (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:463-479), and the proposed delivery re-check skips only an absent or Acknowledged episode (lines 492-500). The resident runner loops indefinitely until its stop file appears (metasystem/internal/steward/runner.go:95-141); replacement is a separate explicit arm/restart operation that stops the process (lines 165-170,403-410,472-501), not an effect of landing a commit. The current alert-channel slice also describes itself as purely additive and says it changes no legacy behavior (metasystem/plans/alert-channel-design.md:989-1044), confirming that no quiescence or retirement deployment contract exists there yet.

## FJA-R3-CLEAR-DRAIN-CRASH-FIXTURE — medium, material=True

CLAIM: No named fixture replays the exact crash boundary that is supposed to prove stop markers cannot leak after narration. The design promises that a crash between NarrateDigest and DrainNarratedStopMarkers leaves a marker that the next tick re-derives, deduplicates, and drains (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:547-560). Fixture 6 executes narration and drain consecutively, while fixture 12 simulates a crash before narration. Neither deliberately succeeds at NarrateDigest, omits the drain, then runs another tick and asserts that the cleared entry remains single and the marker disappears. Without that assertion, an implementation that reports only newly cleared episodes—as opposed to already-cleared episodes whose marker survived—passes the listed fixtures and leaks the marker forever.

EVIDENCE: Fixture 6 calls NarrateDigest and then DrainNarratedStopMarkers in the normal path (metasystem/plans/failed-job-attention-design.md:649-654). Fixture 12's clear half stops after the sweep and before NarrateDigest, then performs a complete tick (lines 679-690). The post-append/pre-drain failure named by the loss proof is absent from both fixtures, and adding it changes a required test assertion.
