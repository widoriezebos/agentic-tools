## Answers to the seven questions

**1. Can a legitimate dispatch be refused or wedged?** In production, no false refusal was found on the four named cases. A missing or unreadable subject is safe: `job read-subject` prints `absent` and writes nothing (`cmd/metasystem/dispatch_verbs.go:1836-1838`), leaving the mktemp'd file at zero bytes, and both call sites guard with `[[ ! -s "$subject_temp" ]]` (`scripts/agents/dispatch.sh:1706`, `:2645`), so the existing missing-artifact path is retained exactly as the brief requires. A first read on a fresh chain is admitted: the candidate root has no record, `authority-check` does not require one (`internal/authority/authority.go:70-81` checks only the caller classification), and with no scoped roots the decision falls through to admitted. A chain whose evidence was collected is unaffected because the collected evidence is what makes the subject computable. A follow-up after a cancelled round is admitted: the cancelled member is skipped by the concurrency rule (`internal/dispatch/read_admission.go:176`) and a cancelled fold contributes no clean observation. The only wedge is F-2, on the retry rather than the first dispatch.
