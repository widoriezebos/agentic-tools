## Voice pass (human ruling: the journey voice)

The human ruled a paper-wide style change: the paper is a journey the narrator and reader take side by side, so the artifact stops narrating itself. "This paper calls" became "we call"; "The paper has not yet shown" became "We have not yet seen"; chapter cross-references became we-voice with tense following direction (past for chapters behind, future for chapters ahead). Kept deliberately: the index page, Chapter 16's closing artifact-facing line, positional "this chapter" phrases, possessives naming built things (Chapter 1's day), locatives, and the first-person-singular practice observations. Round V1 found seven material faults (missed plural forms such as "Chapters 5 and 13 make", two forward references in present tense, one mixed-direction case) and two style improvements; all accepted. Round V2 verified all nine resolved with a clean paper-wide sweep. Material findings: 0.

The human then sharpened the ruling: "I" for the author's own speech-acts (proposing, claiming, offering, deciding scope, leaving open), "we" for the shared journey and for coinages. Nine conversions to "I"/"my" followed, and one borderline case was put to the critic: Chapter 7's "the decisions we reserve for people". Round P1 ruled it stays "we" (a boundary derived with the reader, not a fresh authorial act), verified all nine conversions, and swept the paper clean of misassigned person in both directions. Material findings: 0.
