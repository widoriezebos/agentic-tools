## ERAR-R2-01-SESSION-PROOF — high, material=True

CLAIM: The fold for ERAR-R1-01-STRAY-CRON does not establish a fact that a stray cron cannot supply. Revision 2 says a resolved identity is “the fact a stray cron cannot supply” and reasons that the explicit process pair fails because “a cron entry does not descend from an agent session.” The cited code never proves the target is an agent session: proveCallerDescendsFromTarget succeeds immediately when callerPid equals targetPid, and the fixture driver obtains its own start time and passes `--pid $$ --start-time <that value>`. A cron shell can perform the identical operation. Revision 2 also claims that merely rejecting non-positive fields in ReArmSession preserves the binding for callers bypassing up, but positive caller-provided numbers authenticate nothing. Automatic re-arm must exclude this fallback or bind it to an independently authenticated agent-session capability, and its stray-cron fixture must exercise the explicit-pair route rather than only the no-identity route.

Fixture F2 at metasystem/plans/engine-rebuild-rearm-design.md:671 tests only that identity resolution fails when no identity is supplied, so it cannot catch this bypass.

## ERAR-R2-02-PROVENANCE-SCHEMA — high, material=True

CLAIM: The provenance fold for ERAR-R1-03-PROVENANCE rejects the engine produced by the repository's canonical gate and still does not prove passage through a landing gate. Revision 2 classifies a witness-digest stamp as non-commit drift and says those trees are “never” automatically re-armed. In the shipped workflow, canonical validation invokes the witness gate, the gate exports `METASYSTEM_BUILD_STAMP=witness-<digest>`, and the witness gate copies that engine into the live installation. That routine validated rebuild will therefore wedge exactly as the goal says it must not. Conversely, reachability from a branch only proves ancestry; a directly created commit on that branch passes even though it did not traverse the landing wrapper. The design must define a provenance format that preserves the consumed Git commit alongside the witness digest and identify the landing evidence that makes the commit eligible before implementation.

EVIDENCE: metasystem/plans/engine-rebuild-rearm-design.md:61-63 requires commits that arrived through landing gates, while lines 146-149 reduce that requirement to main-branch ancestry and lines 185-186 explicitly reject the witness stamp. metasystem/scripts/validate-metasystem.sh:570-582 invokes the witness-producing gate. metasystem/scripts/agents/go-gate.sh:492-508 exports the witness stamp and builds with it, and lines 549-556 retain that exported stamp for the final build. metasystem/scripts/agents/witness-gate.sh:128-144 copies the resulting engine into the live path. metasystem/scripts/agents/commit.sh:519-525 shows that the actual landing wrapper records separate landing-provenance trailers, evidence not consulted by the proposed ancestry test.

## ERAR-R2-03-LANDING-REF — high, material=True

CLAIM: The hard-coded `refs/heads/main` residual risk is not acceptable for this all-machines change. It deterministically makes every automatic rebuild on an adopted non-main repository refuse, while the shipped landing tools deliberately land the current branch rather than main. Before building, the design must assign ownership of a fully qualified landing ref—for example, an installation-scoped `metasystem.steward.landing-ref` value seeded by adoption and fleet bootstrap, validated under the arm lock, and failed closed when absent or unresolved—and add a non-main fixture. Deferring configurability leaves an intended fleet population outside the stated outcome.

EVIDENCE: metasystem/plans/engine-rebuild-rearm-design.md:58-63 scopes the order to every machine, but lines 779-782 knowingly defer the non-main failure. metasystem/scripts/agents/land.sh:232-236 and metasystem/scripts/agents/commit.sh:550-556 select the checkout's current branch. metasystem/internal/contract/contract.go:1313-1318 demonstrates an existing, different default-branch resolution rule, confirming that `refs/heads/main` is not a repository-wide invariant. The design provides no authority for choosing between current branch, origin default branch, or a configured landing ref.

## ERAR-R2-04-PRE-MINT-STOP-STAGE — medium, material=True

CLAIM: The typed stage fold leaves an unrepresented destructive pre-mint outcome. Revision 2 defines StageBeforeMint as “nothing changed” and says its rendering means “no runner stopped,” but step 5 calls stopRunnerForReplacement before minting. That function first writes the restart marker and sends termination signals, then can return an error because a signal fails or the runner remains recorded alive. Such an error returns before StageMinted even though the runner may be stopping or dead. The stage contract needs a stopped-or-stop-failed state, or equivalent explicit side-effect data and rendering, so callers do not report a refusal/no-op after changing the runner.

EVIDENCE: metasystem/plans/engine-rebuild-rearm-design.md:235-242 defines StageBeforeMint as unchanged; lines 285-290 place runner stopping before the mint; and lines 614-619 promise that StageBeforeMint stopped no runner. metasystem/internal/steward/runner.go:479-505 writes the stop file and sends SIGTERM and possibly SIGKILL before several possible error returns. The current stage enumeration cannot distinguish this path.

## ERAR-R2-05-VISIBILITY-DURABILITY — medium, material=True

CLAIM: The visibility fold does not specify an implementable three-record durability contract. Decision 2 says both notices are written inside arm under the lock, while Decision 3 says up writes the arming log only after ReArmRebuiltEngine returns; those are different owners and different kill windows. The existing appendArmingLog function is also best-effort and discards every write error, whereas QueueNotification can fail explicitly. Because the identity and notices are separate writes, the deadline parent can kill the worker after the identity mint but before either notice, contradicting “all written before up can fail or be killed” and the reject condition forbidding fewer than three records. The design must choose an owner and ordering, specify notice-write failure outcomes, and either provide a transaction that supports the three-record claim or narrow the invariant to the identity record plus a defined later-health recovery path.

EVIDENCE: metasystem/plans/engine-rebuild-rearm-design.md:289-293 places both notice writes inside arm, but lines 521-525 assign the arming-log write to up after ReArmRebuiltEngine returns. Lines 516-533 and 558-563 claim all three records precede any failure or kill; line 822 makes that a reject condition. metasystem/internal/up/up.go:315-324 ignores directory, open, and write errors. metasystem/internal/steward/intervene.go:299-313 returns pending-notification durability failures. No cited primitive atomically publishes all three records.

## ERAR-R2-06-C16-FIXTURE-SEAM — medium, material=True
