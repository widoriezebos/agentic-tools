# 4. The Mimicry Trap

Thesis: copying human process into agentic work preserves recognizable
forms while obscuring the conditions those forms were meant to
protect.

## An archaeology of ceremony

Standups make otherwise private state visible; sprints batch scarce
attention; estimates allocate expensive labor; code review adds
scrutiny and spreads knowledge; documentation resists memory loss; and
retrospectives create a time for learning. The section will treat each
practice as evidence of a human limit, then name its invariant — the
condition that must remain true — without assuming the form must
survive.

## Why mimicry is attractive

Familiar process looks governable to managers and auditors, and it
keeps humans in the familiar position of reviewing each step. The
chapter will concede that recognizability and accountability matter,
then show why a recognizable ritual is not evidence that its protected
condition still holds.

## The cost of preserving the form

Copied ceremonies create duplicate work, spend computation on reports
no decision uses, and can replace scrutiny with plausible theater. They
also direct attention toward old coordination problems while silent
death, shared machine blind spots, and weak verification go unmanaged.

## Extract the invariant, then choose

For each inherited practice, ask what condition it protected, whether
the new medium still threatens that condition, and what observable
evidence would show that a replacement protects it. The choices are
not always deletion: discard, machine replacement, adaptation, and
deliberate human retention are all legitimate outcomes.

## Four worked cases

A fixed sprint cadence will illustrate discard when it protects no
remaining condition; a status meeting will become a machine-produced
record of live work; and code review will adapt into independent
attempts to find faults tied to test results. A review that elicits
tacit concerns or decides safety, fairness, or accountability will be
retained as a human act until another mechanism demonstrably protects
the same condition.

## What cannot first be extracted

Some ceremonies are where people discover disagreement rather than
execute a rule already known. The method must preserve that human
negotiation, name what is still unknown, and refuse to automate it
merely because the surrounding paperwork can be automated.
