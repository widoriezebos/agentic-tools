# 15. Self-Application

Thesis: a delivery system must survive changing itself, but success at
that test is necessary rather than sufficient evidence for the wider
claims of this paper.

## The distinctive self-change problem

A change to an ordinary application is judged by machinery outside the
application, while a change to a gate, identity check, or authority
rule can weaken the machinery that judges it. Self-application stays a
separate chapter because that circular failure class requires an older,
independent basis for comparison and recovery.

## The bootstrap

The delivery system should use its own records, gates, authority
boundaries, and care loop for changes to itself. A self-change must be
bound to the prior rule set that authorized it, with protected evidence
and a recovery path the proposed change cannot rewrite.

## Rules bind their own maintenance

Workers changing the system do not receive an exemption from the rules
they are changing. Where a rule cannot safely judge its replacement,
an external check or named human authority supplies the boundary rather
than allowing the system to justify itself.

## What self-application can reveal

The test exposes whether a gate can judge a proposed change to that
gate, whether verification survives the work it checks, and whether a
safeguard can catch a regression introduced by its own builder. Passing
shows internal consistency under unusually difficult conditions; it does
not show that the principles fit other domains or serve users well.

## Falsifiability and independent support

The paper will state observations that would count against its
principles: repeated failures across independent applications to reduce
harm or delivery cost, authority boundaries that cannot survive real
organizations, or retained human ceremonies that outperform their
replacements on the same invariant. Support requires reproducible
evidence from different teams, risk levels, applications, and operating
conditions, including negative results and comparisons with simpler
approaches.

## The recursion has a floor

Self-application does not mean self-justification or self-chosen
purpose. The recursion ends in observable reality and accountable human
intent, while both remain open to evidence that the system’s rules or
the intent itself should change.
