# 7. Memory and Coordination

Thesis: ledgers replace meetings; records replace memory; coordination
becomes data.

## The ledger as the center of work
Goals, claims, states, and decisions live in a machine-readable,
history-preserving ledger with atomic transitions. Any worker — or
human — reads the same truth; nobody attends anything. Conflict is
resolved by compare-and-swap and recorded displacement, not by
discussion.

## Handoff by record
Workers are mortal and forgetful by construction, so every unit of
work is resumable from records alone: intent, state, evidence,
next step. The test: kill any worker at any moment; a fresh one must
continue correctly without asking anyone.

## Two audiences, one truth
Machines need structured records; humans need narrative. The system
maintains both from the same substrate: a plain-language running
account of what is happening and why — not dashboards to interrogate
but a story to read — while every sentence remains backed by records.

## Decisions, precedents, and institutional memory
Rulings and their reasons are first-class records. New situations
consult precedent before asking the human again; the system's law
grows the way case law grows, and losing any individual — human or
machine — loses no knowledge.
