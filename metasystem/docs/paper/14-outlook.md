# 13. What Engineering Becomes

Thesis: when applications are cheap, delivery systems are the asset,
and engineering is the discipline of laws, proofs, and judgment.

## The org chart follows the shift
Teams shaped around intent domains and their delivery systems rather
than around components; fewer people, each holding legislator/judge
seats over more delivery capacity; the career ladder reorients from
"writes harder code" to "writes better law, designs sharper proofs,
judges evidence more reliably."

## New scarcities, new skills
Writing enforceable intent; designing discriminating verification;
economic design of spend portfolios; incident and near-miss literacy;
the craft of plain language — because the interface between human and
system is language that must not be misread.

## What we should refuse to lose
Accountability that terminates in humans; the right to understand any
decision from its records; reversibility wherever physics allows;
honest reporting as a non-negotiable norm.

## Open problems
Federation of delivery systems across organizations; portable
standards for evidence and proofs; liability and audit in
machine-built software; economic regulation of runaway spend; how
much of the legislator's seat can itself be assisted without being
surrendered.

## Closing
Restate the thesis in one paragraph: step back to intent, refuse
mimicry, build the system that builds the system — and hold it to
laws it cannot evade, so that human attention is finally spent where
only humans can spend it.
