# 12. A System That Learns

Thesis: experience must change future behavior, but not every lesson
can or should become an automatic refusal.

## Incidents produce candidate lessons

An incident or near-miss first yields a record of observed conditions,
effects, response, and uncertainty. Only then can the system propose a
safeguard — an automatic check that may refuse an action — or a prior
ruling for later human judgment.

## Govern each automatic safeguard

Every proposed safeguard needs a stated minimum amount and quality of
evidence for adoption, a defined scope, an accountable owner, and tests
showing both that it blocks the known failure and permits legitimate
work. Its record must also define how people appeal, how side effects are
observed, and when the safeguard expires, is reviewed, or is repealed.

## Test the safeguard itself

A safeguard can measure the wrong thing, block legitimate work, conflict
with other rules, or remain after the world changes. Cases it must block
and cases it must permit, gradual activation, observation, and
independent review make the safeguard subject to the same skepticism as
the work it judges.

## A tested floor, not one-way progress

Some well-tested safeguards can set a floor that later changes may not
cross unnoticed, such as a dependency restriction or a minimum tested
behavior. This does not mean every measure should only rise or that such
a floor is exempt from appeal and repeal.

## Near-misses widen the evidence base

Events that caused no observed harm can reveal an escape path before
consequences make it expensive. Recording them is useful only when
their uncertainty is preserved and they enter the same governed path
as incidents, rather than producing a new rule by reflex.

## Some lessons cannot become automatic refusals

Value conflicts, rare situations that a fixed set of tests cannot
anticipate, weak signals, and situations without a reliable machine test
may resist automatic enforcement. Such lessons remain as warnings,
questions, or prior rulings routed to a named human authority, with the
cost of repeated judgment acknowledged.

## Repair, learn, and revisit

Recovery may restore the previous safe version or make a new corrective
change according to user harm and reversibility; learning follows rather
than delaying containment. The loop closes only when the resulting
safeguard, record, or human practice is later checked against production
outcomes and revised when it fails.

## The change, continued

Suppose the expiry release mishandles clocks just before daylight-saving
time changes. The incident may justify a tested safeguard against
local-time comparisons, while a subtler question about acceptable
lockout burden remains a prior human ruling with an owner and review date
rather than an automatic ban.
