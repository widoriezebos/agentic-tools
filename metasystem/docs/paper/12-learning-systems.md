# 12. A System That Learns

Thesis: experience must change future behavior, but not every lesson
can or should become an automatic refusal.

## Incidents produce candidate lessons

An incident or near-miss first yields a record of observed conditions,
effects, response, and uncertainty. Only then can the system propose a
guard — an automatic check that may refuse an action — or a precedent
for later human judgment.

## Guard governance

Every proposed guard needs an evidence threshold for adoption, a
defined scope, an accountable owner, and tests showing both that it
blocks the known failure and permits legitimate work. Its record must
also define how people appeal, how side effects are observed, and when
the guard expires, is reviewed, or is repealed.

## Test the guard itself

A guard can encode a bad proxy, produce false positives, accumulate
into contradiction, or remain after the world changes. Known-bad and
known-good cases, staged activation, monitoring, and independent review
make the guard subject to the same skepticism as the work it judges.

## Progress that cannot silently regress

Some well-tested safeguards can set a floor that later changes may not
cross unnoticed, such as a dependency restriction or a minimum tested
behavior. The chapter will call this a ratchet, but will reject the idea
that every metric should only rise or that such a floor is exempt from
appeal and repeal.

## Near-misses widen the evidence base

Events that caused no observed harm can reveal an escape path before
consequences make it expensive. Recording them is useful only when
their uncertainty is preserved and they enter the same governed path
as incidents, rather than producing a new rule by reflex.

## Some lessons cannot compile into refusal

Value conflicts, rare open-world patterns, weak signals, and situations
without a reliable machine test may resist automatic enforcement. Such
lessons remain as warnings, questions, or precedents routed to a named
human authority, with the cost of repeated judgment acknowledged.

## Repair, learn, and revisit

Recovery may roll back or repair forward according to user harm and
reversibility; learning follows rather than delaying containment. The
loop closes only when the resulting guard, record, or human practice is
later checked against production outcomes and revised when it fails.

## The change, continued

Suppose the expiry release mishandles clocks just before daylight-saving
time changes. The incident may justify a tested guard against local-time
comparisons, while a subtler question about acceptable lockout burden
remains a human precedent with an owner and review date rather than a
mechanical ban.
