### 1.3 The message contract

Every message to a person is one of two things: a question whose options are solutions, or a delivery. A question has, in this order, at most nine lines and about 600 characters:

1. **Subject and event.** The goal's name as words (`featureName`, `report.go:276-278`), then what happened and when, in one sentence. For a question about the machine or the lane the subject is "The seat on ui" or "The landing lane".
2. **Why it matters**, one sentence, when it is not obvious from line 1: what is blocked, what is at stake.
3. **Options**, two or three, the recommended one first with ★. Each: a label of one to three words, then what it does, what it costs or risks, and how it is undone (the real verb, or "the question stays open"), in one line. The label is exactly what the person replies with, and the last line ("reply: A / B") is generated from the labels, so the two can never differ. This line is the impact statement R-143 asks for, shown before the choice; the confirmation repeats the undo.
4. **If you do nothing:** what the code makes true, and when the one reminder comes (U4).
5. **How to answer:** "Tap a button, or reply: <label> / <label>." Without buttons (no provider support, the pane, or the one-time code switched on): "Reply: <label> / <label>." Generated from the labels.

What is not on the phone: facts, record paths, budget tuples, job ids, the prepared brief's path. They live in `question show Q`, in the browser's Decisions page, and in the question record.

The contract is enforced where the question is made, not where it is rendered, and only for the producers this page migrates: an `AskRequest` with `Contract: true` is refused by `channel.Ask` without a decision sentence, without at least one option that has a consequence, with a decision over 280 runes or an option consequence over 200 runes, or with a decision or consequence that contains a 10-hex-digit run, a path under `/Users/`, the word `pid`, or `generation N` (one regular expression, tested). The producer sees the refusal and writes a shorter, plainer question. A contract question is never truncated in flight. Producers not yet migrated (the lane's policy question, `policy_question.go:385`; the process-change questions) keep today's `Ask`, text and rendering until their own units move them; nothing that posts today stops posting.

The confirmation contract: `<Label> it is. <What the machine did, in the goal's words> at <HH:MM>. <What happens next and when you hear again>. Undo: <the verb from the option line>.` The refusal contract: `I could not <act>: <plain reason>. The question above is still open; <what makes its options work again>.`

## 2. Threat model and rabbit-hole risks

Threat model: our own agents and operators make mistakes; nobody attacks. Two things must still hold under that model: a message from anyone but the configured human never makes the machine act (`inbox.go:111` stands); a message to a closed question never acts twice (R-129).

Rabbit holes, each kept out by name:
- A second bot or a second transport. One bot, the ledger inbox, first commit wins (old page D8) stands.
- A notification redesign of the browser. The browser shows the same question; its buttons are a future iteration.
- Perfect coverage before step 1. Step 1 is the questions that exist today; coverage grows in U3 and U5 and in the approved units of other goals.
- Re-cutting the channel goal's U3 into something bigger. This page takes its gate (U3d) and leaves its asks (U3a-U3c) where they are, under this contract.
- A free-text message verb (decision 19). Only questions and deliveries.
- A parallel store of answers or acts. The question record is the only store; the act's own ledger entry is its proof.
- Understanding natural language. Only option words bind; anything else gets the "I did not catch that" line.
- Defaults that act for the person (NO HAL9000). None in step 1.

The command's exit and outcome word are not the proof that the act ran: `work revise` can admit the correction and still return `refused` for the next stop it meets, or `in-progress` while the correction runs (`intent_work.go:1602-1622`, `intent_build_outcome_test.go:131`). The proof is read from the act's own records after the command returns, in two steps per act class. Accepted: the request is retained (a revision → the run record's `revisions[]` entry with this person and reason, written before admission at `unit_revise.go:222`; a retry → the step's `RetryBy`, `unit_step_failure.go:167`; a size acceptance → the round's `SizeAcceptedBy`, `intent_unit_questions.go:187`; a goal verb → the goal file's history line with the act's opid, which for a goal verb is also started). Accepted but not started (the launch refused for budget, `unit_launch.go:68`, or host load, `hostcapacity.go:89`): `Answer.Phase = accepted`, the question stays open, and the thread gets `<Label> it is; it waits: <the hold in plain words>. When that is fixed, reply <Label> again.` Nothing restarts by itself: an open question also keeps the steward from starting a seat for the goal (`seat_ladder.go:82-83`, `AskedOpen`). The person's fresh reply is a new message: it is recorded as a new answer (`Phase: recorded`), and the executor runs the same act, which the verb treats as its own replay (R-129) and which is then judged started or accepted again. A replayed revision already calls the continuation (`unit_revise.go:254`). A replayed retry or size acceptance does not today: the retry admission marks the run running before anything launches (`unit_step_failure.go:178`), so `work review` returns early as "still running" (`intent_selection.go:770`), and the first size acceptance clears the `build-size` outcome that selects its branch (`unit_build_outcome.go:159`, `intent_unit_review.go:73`). A replay with nothing retained is the verb's ordinary unchanged result. Started: `Answer.Phase = acted`, the confirmation line is posted, the question closes (an untyped question by the executor with `closed: <label> done at HH:MM`; a typed question by the act's own record, `unit_stop.go:121-157`), and a later stop the same command reported is left to its own producer, which asks its own question. Not accepted: the attempt is appended to `Question.Attempts []Attempt{At, Label, Outcome, Message}` with the inbox record's identity, that record is marked handled, `Answer` is cleared so the matcher (`inbox.go:540`, `q.Answer == nil`) accepts the next new message, the question stays open with its options, and the refusal line is posted with what makes the options work again.
