## The common cause, measured

Twenty of the twenty-two material read findings of 2026-10-12 have one shape: a reader reconstructs "what is the lane doing" from some of the records and gets a case wrong — the silent-agent minutes from a step's start instead of its end (194), the singleton judged without the trunk-red hold (194), critic tokens matched on a record shape that does not exist (204), health calling the lane paused from loop stops (228), the steward's view built without the fingerprint source so the barren count read 0 (231), a merge judged stale from "main moved" alone and then from an abandoned record of another merge (212-215), deploy copying resume's ownership predicate with one case missing (226), a remedy mapper guessing the verb for every unknown state (230). The three other recurring causes are the same shape one level down: records that nobody retires (seventeen loop stops open since 2026-10-10, questions re-asked from facts read before a clear: 158, 159, 228, 232), verbs that fail invisibly (a nil-pointer panic in a refusal path, a job id stored before the launch that never came, "waiting since an unrecorded time": 233), and copies where one function should exist (resume and deploy, 226; three stale test expectations after one behaviour change, 206/211). Patching readers one at a time produced more readers; the steward and the recovery verbs of 2026-10-12 are readers of this kind too.

## Decisions

- D1 One state record. The lane keeps `artifacts/agents/landing/state.json`: lane (running, paused, stopped; since; by; reason), agent (session; word: idle, stepping, step-process-gone or ended; the step; since), batch (id; execution: prepared, running or closed with its since; admission: open or sealed with its since — batch.go:49 keeps these as two independent facts: the gate starts execution while admission stays open, the full proof seals admission while execution stays running; members; base), one entry per submission in the lane, keyed by the submission's own identity (goal, SHA and the submission's ordinal in the queue, counted from the queue line that recorded it), so a goal with two hand-ins has two entries and a SHA re-submitted after a return has two entries too, as queue.go:204-226 and health.go:193-227 keep today, resolution (goal, state, job, since), proof (attempt, scope, state, since, load at start), main (commit, proven green/red/unproven, since, open incidents), questions (id, premise, since), holds (barren count, selection pending, since), engine (stamp), version. Every field with a state carries its since, and since is one of two things, never confused: a transition time (written by the transition that produced the state) or an observation time (the first tick on which the builder saw the state in a raw record that carries no time, marked `observed` with the record's name); the builder never invents a time, and the describer says "seen since 21:02 (the batch record carries no running time)" for an observation. Step 1 instruments the writers that lack a time today: the batch's running and closed transitions (batch.go:830 and its close) and the pause record's revocation; a commit that reached main outside the lane's pushes (queue.go:464-476, no landing time exists) stays an honest observation. The record changes only through transitions: `Transition(install, kind, from, to, evidence)` appends one line to `events.jsonl` (time, kind, from, to, by, evidence) and rewrites state.json atomically under the state file's own lock (`state.lock`) and never the installation lock, which has no reentrancy (internal/lock/file.go) and which the calling verb may already hold; a transition from a state the record is not in is refused and recorded as a defect event, never silently applied.
- D2 One predicate library, `internal/landing/state`: merge ownership (abandoned, owned by a running job, settled), goal admissibility (eligible, cap, policy, held by an incident, person's seal), a person's act versus an agent's (provenance and enrollment), a question's premise (current, gone, unknown), a stop's owner (person, loop). Status, health, the steward, the keeper, the questions and every landing verb call these; an audit test fails on a second implementation of any predicate's name or of its decision table.
- D3 One lifecycle table. Every record kind declares in one table when it retires: a stop closes when its subject landed, returned or was superseded or its attempt is not current; a question withdraws when its premise is gone; a fix record ends done or abandoned; a returned entry leaves the views after the day window; a result older than the retention stays in the file but out of the views. The keeper applies the table on its tick as transitions; health lists any record past its rule as a defect row ("a loop stop of 2026-10-10 survived its subject"), so the table rots visibly.
- D4 Verbs fail visibly. Every landing verb runs under one guard: a panic becomes a refusal with the stack in an incident record and exit 2 (never a silent exit); a job id is stored in a record only after the launch record exists (launch first, record second, both under the lock); every wait names its start; a verb that changes a record emits the transition, so the events file is the lane's complete history.
- D5 One describer. `Describe(state)` renders the status headline, the health facts, the steward's rows, the questions' first lines and every remedy from the state record and one table (state → words, the intent verb, what a person may do), never from the raw records. This subsumes lane-recovery U4 (the wording rule): the words live in one place, and the audit walks the table, not the producers.
- D6 Migration in three steps, each usable. Step 1: the state record is a shadow built by ONE builder — a pure projection of the raw observation that status already makes (readStatus split into observe and health assembly; health compares the shadow file, read first, against the projection of the same observation, so there is no second reader and no cycle through status.go:193; the shadow is captured at the start of the one observation boundary the status, health and steward verbs share, BEFORE lane.BuildView reads the registration and pause records, and the observation is coherent only when a fingerprint of the shadow's input records (one list in one place) is unchanged across the read — an incoherent observation is re-taken, never compared or published — and a publication refuses, under the state file's lock, to overwrite a record whose version or input fingerprint moved since the observation, so an older refresh never overwrites a newer one and the comparison never manufactures a disagreement from its own read order; an observation's since carries over from the previous record while the identity and word are unchanged, so an unchanged observed state never drifts; an observation with problems (an unreadable record, Status.Problems) never replaces the last complete shadow — it is recorded as a failed observation the describer shows beside the shadow's age) — from today's records (the last place that re-derives), through the read-only readers only (the read-only status path: ProveSeams.ReadOnly true, so no RefreshMerge and no installation lock, status.go:471-477, fix.go:287) and rewritten under the state file's own lock on every keeper tick — at the tick's entry, before the host lock and whatever the tick's outcome (lane/agent.go:232-259) — and after every verb; health and the steward read the shadow; the raw readers stay authoritative in step 1, and the comparison runs in two places: a test compares the shadow with the raw-record readers on the fixtures of every lane test and fails on any disagreement, and health compares live on every call (the shadow read first, then the read-only raw readers; no lock beyond the state file's) and prints every difference — a word, a since or a payload such as a batch's members or a question's premise, named by the one difference function the rebuild event uses too — as a defect row ("shadow: lane running since 21:40; records: paused since 21:52 by the person") and the keeper writes the same difference as a `drift` event on its tick — so a crash between a raw write (pause.go:69) and the shadow refresh is visible on the next health call, never silently repaired: the rebuild that follows records what it corrected. Step 2: the verbs write transitions directly and the shadow builder becomes the consistency check that runs on every tick and reports drift as a defect row. Step 3: the derived readers (readStatus's reconstruction, the keeper's own view, the steward's view) are deleted; status, health, steward and questions read the record only.

## Step 1 and what is deferred (R-121)
Step 1 is U1 and U2: the state record with its transitions and events, the shadow builder, health and the steward reading the shadow, and the agreement test over every existing lane fixture. After it, `landing health --json` carries the state record, every "since" is a real time, and any disagreement between the shadow and the old readers is a visible defect row. Deferred in order: U3 the predicate library and its audit (resume, deploy, the steward and the batch switch to it); U4 the lifecycle table applied by the keeper; U5 the verb guard and launch-before-record; U6 the describer (absorbing lane-recovery U4); then steps 2 and 3 as their own goal once step 1 has run a night.

## Threat model
(1) The shadow disagrees with the old readers and is believed: in step 1 nothing acts on the shadow alone (the steward keeps its own view until the agreement test has been green for a night); disagreements are rows, not acts. (2) The events file grows: one file per day, retired by the lifecycle table. (3) Lock contention on every verb: the transition is one small write under the lock the verbs already take. (4) A transition refused because the record lags reality (a crashed verb left the record behind): the refusal is recorded and the shadow builder repairs the record from the raw records on the next tick in step 1; in step 2 the guard of D4 makes that case an incident. (5) The design itself becomes a reader of readers: exactly two places compare (the fixture test and health's live comparison), both report and neither acts; everything else reads the record. (6) A raw record that carries no time: the record says `observed`, never a guessed time; a row for every observed field survives until its writer is instrumented (step 1 does the four named).
