## 2. Decisions

- **D1 One receiver per bot.** `receiveToInbox` (`inbox.go:39-87`) holds the bot's host lock from before `Receive` (`:43`) to after the last `Confirm` (`:82`). The lock is `<home>/host/channel/<bot>.flock`, `<home>` the board's home (`internal/board/card.go:192-208`), taken with `lock.TryExclusive` (`internal/lock/file.go:18-19`); held elsewhere, the pass receives nothing this time, as a 409 does today (`inbox.go:44-46`). `<bot>` is `channel.BotKey(dest)`: the provider name and the first 12 hex of the SHA-256 of the API base and the bot's identity, which is the token's part before the colon when it has one (a Telegram token's public bot id), else the destination's channel id (the fake's tokens carry a per-listener suffix and no colon, `internal/channel/fake/fake.go:44-48`). Never the secret. A caller that already holds the lock says so with `PollConfig.BotHeld` (the poller, D2). This covers every receive and confirm path: the tick (`poll.go:165-166`), the two waits (`channel_verbs.go:351`, `:465`) and the poller all go through `receiveToInbox`, the only caller of both provider methods.
- **D2 The poller is a role of the steward runner.** Built like the bridge role (`internal/steward/bridge_role.go:20-28`, `:101-160`). Every runner cycle, before the tick, a runner whose installation can check a reply (`phase.Load(top, true)`, `phase.go:151-175`; `receiveConfigured`, `inbox.go:31-33`) tries the bot's lock. The winner keeps it, writes its pid into it, and runs the poll loop in a goroutine of its own process. Its line is printed when it changes, beside the bridge's (`runner.go:362-368`): "channel poller serving bot telegram-3f2a... (this steward, pid N)", "channel poller held by pid N", or "channel poller not serving: <reason>". It runs at the helm. The loop: one `channel.ReceiveOnce` pass, which is `receiveToInbox` with `BotHeld` and without the opening flush, on a long poll of `channel.long-poll-sec` (default 25; `DestinationConfig.LongPoll`, passed by `telegram.Receive` to `updates` at `telegram.go:237`, whose HTTP timeout is the long poll plus 15 seconds, `:292-293`). A provider without a long poll (Slack) is called every `channel.long-poll-sec` seconds. Busy (409 from an older engine or another host): wait 2 seconds. Any other error: wait 2 seconds, doubling to 60, and say it in the role's line. The loop's clock and sleep are injected (R-104-m1e). Settings are read when the role starts; a change takes effect at the runner's next re-arm.
- **D3 Receipt never waits for an installation.** The poller's pass never takes an installation's channel lock (`poll.go:83`). What it shares with the installation's own passes is the notice ledger (`fleet/notices.json`, `inbox.go:351-464`). Two locks guard it, and no lock is ever held across a provider call. The data lock, `notices.json.lock`, is held with `lock.Exclusive` (`internal/lock/file.go:14`) only for a read-modify-write of the file, never during a post: `queueNotice` and `dropNotice` (`inbox.go:383-428`; the verified commit's `dropNotice` at `:249`) take it for milliseconds. The flush lock, `notices.flush.lock`, is taken by `flushNotices` with `TryExclusive` for its whole run, posts included (`inbox.go:448`); a flusher that finds it busy returns at once and leaves the work to the holder. Inside, the flusher reads the pending notices under the data lock, releases it, posts each one, and then under the data lock again re-reads the file and moves each posted record from pending to posted, keeping what others queued meanwhile. So the receiver's `dropNotice` waits only for another file write, never for an HTTP post, and two flushers never post one notice twice. The poller's pass is then: read (the long poll returns as an update arrives), acknowledge, commit (one ledger publish per update, 2 seconds measured), confirm, hand the record to delivery (D4), read again. Delivery runs in its own goroutine and never blocks the loop. A second tap is read as soon as the first batch's commits end, not when its act ends.

  Budget, tap to result, for one tap (evidence or marked assumption):

  | Step | Time | Basis |
  |---|---|---|
  | tap to read | under 1 s | assumption: a parked long poll returns as the update arrives (Bot API) |
  | acknowledge | under 1 s | assumption: one HTTP call, made before the commit |
  | commit | about 2 s | measured once: 21:30:01 to 21:30:03 |
  | confirm, hand over | under 1 s | assumption: one HTTP call |
  | owner's pass starts | under 1 s | assumption: process start and settings read |
  | owner reads the ledger tip | 1 to 3 s | assumption: one fetch (`goal.ReadChannelInboxAtTip`, `goal/channel.go:784-792`) |
  | answer recorded | about 2 s | assumption: one ledger publish, as the commit |
  | the act | unmeasured | `work revise ... --answer Q` admits and starts a launch; assumption 5 to 20 s |
  | line and buttons | under 2 s | assumption: two HTTP calls |

  So the acknowledgement comes within about 2 seconds of the tap, and the result line within about 30 seconds when the act returns within 20. That is a target, not a promise: when no result is posted 30 seconds after delivery, the poller posts once "Continue is being applied; the result follows here." (D4). The rollout measures the real figures (section 7).
- **D4 Delivery to the owning installation, and what the person is told.** After a pass commits a verified record with a `replyTo`, the poller finds its owners: the host's armed seats (`hostSeats`, `bridge_role.go:79-98`) whose question store has a question with that thread id, thread root or notice id (`channel.OwnsReply(root, replyTo)`, the predicate of `inbox.go:547`, any state). For each owner it starts `<installation>/bin/metasystem question settle --json` with the installation as its folder (the engine path as `internal/missionrunner/host.go:361-366` names it, without `METASYSTEM_BIN`) and with `METASYSTEM_OWNER_LINEAGE` removed, so the answer is recorded under the owner's machine and lineage `steward` (`phase.go:197-207`). One child per installation at a time; a record for a busy installation starts it once more after it ends. A child that reports busy (the owner's own pass holds its channel lock) is started again every 2 seconds, at most 15 times. The person is told, once, in the record's thread, by the poller:
  - Applied or refused: nothing from the poller; the owner's pass posts its line (D7).
  - No line after 30 seconds: "Continue is being applied; the result follows here."
  - Failed, or busy 15 times, the owner not at the helm (`helm.Active`, `internal/helm/helm.go:130`): "Continue is recorded, but it could not be applied yet: <plain reason>. This machine tries again at its next steward pass, about every 10 minutes; or tap Continue again." Both are true: the owner's inbox pass (D6) matches the record unless it was handled, and an answer that was recorded but not acted on is finished by any pass with a provider (`unfinishedAnswer`, `poll.go:421-428`, run at `:122-135`, which reaches `settleChoice` through `:293-297`).
  - The same at the helm: "Continue is recorded, but it could not be applied: <plain reason>. This machine is at the helm and does not retry by itself; tap Continue again to try once more." At the helm no duty with a provider runs (`runner.go:385-391`; settle-replies has none, `poll.go:293`). The retry path that remains is the person's next tap: a new record, a new delivery, and its pass finishes the unfinished answer first.
  - A record no seat on this host owns: nothing is posted; it stays in the ledger inbox for the fleet's other readers.
  - An unthreaded answer (a typed token, matched at `inbox.go:589-600`): not delivered at once. The owner's inbox pass matches it within one steward cycle, about 10 minutes; at the helm the settle-replies duty records it within one cycle (it needs no provider for a question that is not a contract, `poll.go:299-309`).

  A tap is the person's act, so it is applied at the helm too (R-142-m1e); the act's own verb still checks its proof (`--answer Q`, the-answer-is-the-act D4). The acknowledgement says "<label> received" (`answerCallbackQuery` with `text`, `telegram.go:258`).
- **D5 The inbox pass and its verb.** `question settle` is a hidden entry in the command table (`cmd/metasystem/intent_table.go:545-562`, launcher `internal/steward/channel_poller.go`). It runs `phase.Settle(ctx, root)`: the tick's duty without receiving, keeping its split-approval and landed retries (`phase.go:233-246`). It posts undelivered questions (`poll.go:100-121`), finishes answers (`:122-135`), matches the ledger inbox and acts (`:168`), and posts every queued line before it returns (D7). It bounds provider calls with a one-minute context as the goal-less wait does (`channel_verbs.go:464`), and prints one JSON line: dispositions, undelivered, busy, the error in plain words. It is idempotent (R-129-ui; `unfinishedAnswer`, `poll.go:421-428`; the handled record after D10).
- **D6 The tick stops receiving.** `PollConfig` gains `Receive bool`; `runPoll` receives only when it is set (`poll.go:165`). `phase.Run` does not set it: the tick's channel duty becomes `phase.Settle` and is recorded as `inbox-pending`, said "inbox pending" (`runner.go:460`, `duties.go:17`, `:25`). The two waits set it (`channel_verbs.go:351`, `:465`) and stay under D1's lock, so on a host without a poller (a fixture bed, a host with no armed runner) `question wait` still receives.
- **D7 The line goes out in the pass that acted.** A sending pass calls `flushNotices` once more after `matchInbox` (`poll.go:168-169`).
- **D8 A settled question answers, and says who settled it.** `matchInbox`'s early return (`inbox.go:490-498`) also counts a contract question closed in the last day (`ClosedAt`, `question.go:104`), as `recentlyActed` counts an acted one (`:655-658`). `UnitStopAct` gains `By` (`unit_stop.go:82-93`): the person's name when the act was a person's, else empty. Three writers set it. The finding paths of `work revise` know the person (`cmd/metasystem/intent_work.go:1816-1819`, written at `:1842` under the check at `:1839`; `intent_selection.go:916-925`, written at `:953` under `:948`); they run only when the revision carries findings from a completed read (`internal/launch/unit_revise.go:199-201`). The build-hold closure, the path of tonight's incident, writes its act from the run record (`cmd/metasystem/intent_unit_questions.go:195`) when a later round exists; it takes `By` from the retained revision that started that later round, `record.Revisions` (`internal/launch/unit_run.go:69-71`) with `After` equal to the closed round's number (`unit_revise.go:199`), its `Person` (`:32`). `reconcileUnitStopQuestions` passes `By` to `closeAt` (`unit_stop.go:157`). The question keeps it in a new field, `ClosedActor` (`json:"closedActor,omitempty"`), beside `ClosedAt` (`question.go:104`). It is not `UnitStop.ClosedBy`, which stays the successful act's id (`question.go:722-723`, `unit_stop.go:18`). The settled line names the actor: "This was settled at 21:38 by Wido: the retained build hold was continued." A close with no recorded actor keeps today's words.
- **D9 The poller stops in bounded time and hands over cleanly.** One generic fail-safe for the role's close. Close sets the stop mark and cancels the pass's context: the long poll ends, and every later provider call of the pass fails at once, so nothing is confirmed after close and every unconfirmed update stays with Telegram. Close then waits at most 30 seconds (injected clock) for the pass. A pass stuck in a ledger publish cannot be cancelled (`txn.go:690`, `:181`); Close then returns without releasing, and the pass's goroutine releases the lock itself when the publish returns. The runner calls Close only as `RunLoop` returns (deferred, as the bridge's at `runner.go:313-316`), and the `steward run` verb then ends the process (`cmd/metasystem/steward_verbs.go:339`), so in the worst case the kernel releases the lock. An update committed but not confirmed comes again to the next holder, whose commit loses to the first and confirms (`inbox.go:225-241`). This replaces the bridge's unbounded wait (`bridge_role.go:168-172`) for this role only.
- **D10 Handled after the answer is safe.** One generic fail-safe for the matcher. A chosen contract reply is marked handled only after `recordMatch` has written the answer (`inbox.go:716`), not before (`:569-572`): `recordMatch` takes the mark as a step, after the answer write and before the existing failure point `matched` (`:719`) and the advance (`:722`). A reply that earns only a line is marked handled after its notice is queued, as today (`:565` before `:569`). A new failure point `contract-chosen` sits between the choice and `recordMatch`. A retry after a failure there matches again and acts once. A retry after a failure at `matched`: every pass runs unfinished answers before it matches (`poll.go:122-135` before `:168`), so the answer is acted on once and its line posted once; the record is handled, so the matcher posts nothing more. Only a crash in the gap between the answer write and the handled write leaves the record unhandled after the act; the next match then posts one more true line, "Already done at 21:29 (Continue)." (`inbox.go:630-631`).

## 4. Questions answered

1. Where does the poller run? In the steward runner that holds the bot's lock (D2). Rejected: a separate process started by `system up`; it needs its own revival and re-arm, which the runner has. Rejected: a launchd job (Wido: no cron).
2. How do two hosts agree? Today there is one host (`~/.metasystem/armed-checkouts.jsonl`). Within a host the lock decides (D1). Across hosts Telegram serves one `getUpdates` (409, `telegram.go:115-118`) and the first commit wins (`inbox.go:280-290`). Rejected: a lease on the inbox cursor; there is no cursor (`inbox.go:43` passes an empty offset; `fleet/cursor.json` has no writer), and a ledger lease costs a publish per renewal. Rejected: a designated host in the committed settings, for a case that does not exist yet.
3. Mixed versions. An installation on an older engine still receives in its tick and its waits without the lock. Its `getUpdates` ends the poller's long poll with 409 (busy, D2: retried after 2 seconds), or reads first. Either way the record is committed before it is confirmed and nothing is lost; a tap an old tick reads gets today's behaviour (no words, applied at the owner's tick). The cutover ends when all three engines run U1 (section 7).
4. What if the poller dies? The kernel releases the lock; another runner takes it at its next cycle. Telegram keeps unconfirmed updates (hypothesis: 24 hours, its documented retention).
5. Why start the owner's engine? The act must run as the owner's machine with the owner's engine; `settleChoice` runs `os.Executable()` (`poll.go:410`). Rejected: a wake file the owner's runner watches; that runner is inside a 2 to 4 minute tick most of the time.
6. The verb's name: proposed `question settle`, hidden. Verb naming is Wido's call. Rejected: `question apply`, `channel settle`.

## 9. Rejected alternatives

- A long poll inside the tick's channel duty. The tick runs every 8 to 10 minutes; a long poll there only blocks it.
- A shorter tick, or a channel-only fast tick. Every installation would still poll the one bot, and the owner would still wait for its own pass.
- Each runner polls in its own goroutine. Three pollers on one bot end each other's long polls all day.
- The poller takes the installation's channel lock to commit. It is held through acts (Q7).
- Reading past an uncommitted batch with Telegram's offset. The offset confirms every earlier update (Bot API), so a crash would lose replies.
- A wake file to the owner's runner (Q5). The poller applying the act in its own process: the wrong engine and machine.
- A lease in the ledger, or a designated host in the committed settings (Q2).
- Delivering every record to every seat on the host: a failing seat would speak for a question it does not own.
- Delivering unthreaded answers at once. They are typed tokens, rarer than taps; one cycle is stated (D4).
- A retry loop in the poller that applies a failed tap later by itself. At the helm nothing else would run it; the honest line and the person's next tap are enough for this goal (R-124-m1u).
- Removing the buttons when a tap meets a settled question. It needs a provider in the settle-replies pass, which runs without one by design (the-answer-is-the-act D3). Named for later.
- A second host's poller. Named, not built.
