## What this goal delivers

The lane consumes its four policies at the decision they govern. It records the selected goal/commit pairs, asks for the specific human act at `person`, and accepts that act while the helm, pause or question remains standing. An agent cannot obtain that authority by copying the command or by being admitted through the helm.

1. `landing.batch` selects everything eligible at selection time, at most N, or a person's explicit list. Proof and push reject work outside that selection.
2. `landing.proof` asks before each new full execution at `person`; merge gates remain automatic within an admitted selection. Reusing green evidence spends no execution.
3. `landing.on-red` can stop immediately after recording a red, before classification executions, retries or returns. Automatic returns stop on repeated failures and never exceed two per goal.
4. `landing.trunk-red` asks even for an incident's fix goal at `person`. The existing exception command records a person's word for one goal/commit and the observed incidents.
5. Status, the board, the keeper and `question show` read the same recorded subjects and remedies.

**Step 1 is U1: an enforced automatic batch cap.** Three eligible goals with cap two produce a recorded two-member batch; a proof of all three cannot start. This works through today's run/prove/push loop before the remaining person consumers are built. At this increment `person` holds selection and names the pending consumer; it never degrades to `auto`. U2a supplies human selection and subject questions; U2b supplies execution under the helm, pause and drain; U3–U5 attach the other decisions to those same owners. Units are build increments; the goal is handed in only after all six are finished and read.

The threat is mistakes, stale observations, races and interrupted processes on a trusted host. No new service, generic authorization engine, grant type, counter store or question store. This page authorizes design only. “No HAL 9000” means advisory state cannot veto a proved person's explicit act. It does not authorize guessing a destructive target, taking another writer's tree, impersonation, or an unproven push.

Add **`plain.SelectBatch`, `ReadBatch` and `CheckBatch`**, with one atomically replaced lane-local **`batch.json`** under the queue lock. Fields: id, registered lane identity, original base main commit, ordered `GoalSHA` members, selector (effective policy with source, or human act provenance), created-at, state (`prepared`, `running`, `closed`), and closure reason. Existing queue outcomes remain authoritative; the batch is a selection, not another queue. Preserve its final selection until the next batch replaces it; proof/result/push records retain its id and members for history.

The keeper's observer and explicit `landing run` call SelectBatch before an agent launches. Fetch main outside the queue lock, capture its commit, then under the lock reread queue membership, holds and relevant policies. Revalidate the registration before writing. `auto` selects all eligible waiting entries in queue order; decimal N selects the first N. Compare the decimal cap to the available count without narrowing it to a machine integer: the policy branch deliberately accepts arbitrarily large decimal caps. No arbitrary cap is added. At `person`, prepare the same proposal and ask; prepared alone is not admitted. Status reads this record; it does not start work or secretly select a batch on a status read.

An automatic selection becomes running at the first merge-gate/full-proof admission, after a last policy and membership check. A changed cap before this boundary reselects; after it, the cap applies to the next batch. A later batch-policy change, including `person`, governs the next selection. A later proof or on-red policy change governs its next effect independently; it does not ask again for each merge gate. New hand-ins never join a running batch, including a rebuild after a failed push.

CheckBatch inspects the candidate's first-parent merge chain, anchored at the captured base (or the freshly fetched main when rebuilding). Each second parent must be an exact selected SHA in selection order, with no unselected queued SHA newly contained transitively. Mere membership of `Result.Goals`, a tree hash, or a caller-supplied batch id is insufficient. Main's already landed history is excluded. Baseline and per-merge gates may cover a prefix; a full batch proof/push must account for every selected member as included, already on fetched main, returned, superseded, or conflict-held by its existing queue record. No silent omission. Unknown topology or containment is not permission. Preserve 1a's legal main/record refreshes; do not require a byte-identical base forever.

A returned member may leave the remaining selection; a batch-conflicting member waits through its existing `after` record. Both changes derive from queue outcomes, do not admit replacements, and preserve the original proof budget. A newer tip supersedes only that goal/commit's selection authority; the old tip cannot be proven/pushed as the current hand-in. Under `person`, the new tip requires a new selection act. Finish or explicitly replace the unused selection only after establishing no proof, regeneration or agent owns its tree; never reset a live checkout to make a command fit.

A push retry retains the same selected pairs and proof budget while rebasing the *batch assembly* onto fetched main by merges, never rebasing the goal branches. Record its current proof commit/tree in Running/Result; the original batch base remains provenance. Close the batch after all members are terminal for this batch and no execution remains, or after a confirmed push accounts for them. If a push succeeded but local recording failed, reconcile containment on the next fetch before another launch; do not push twice or lose the batch's identity.

The drain design's `AdvanceDrain` additionally requires no active prepared/admitted selection with unresolved members and no running batch operation before recording held. A stale empty proposal can close through this same reconciliation. Unknown batch state prevents declaring drained, not a proved person's explicit action. Preserve the drain design's distinction between unknown progress and a fatal keeper observation error.

**Policy integration.** Reuse **policy branch** `config.ResolvePolicy` and `inv.policyParams`/`policyReaders`; supply the canonical registered lane as owner and preserve the original calling checkout so seat environment variables cannot override lane values. The command layer injects this reader into plain lane operations and keeper composition; no import from config back into the lane, and no duplicate precedence. Use a small **`plain.PolicyDecision`** over a resolved value/error, the requested lane operation and validated act scope. It answers proceed, hold with the required act, or proceed with a person's advisory warning. It selects no tests and owns no storage. Its production callers are selection, proof execution, red continuation, return, hand-in and push. Each call reads just the policy it needs, never all settings as an admission prerequisite.
