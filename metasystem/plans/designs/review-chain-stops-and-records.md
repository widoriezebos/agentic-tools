## Wido's binding words from the plan

> “17 critic rounds is nuts” and “be critical on the review cycle”.

> “No HAL 9000”: a person's act always takes effect; the machinery may say why it would have decided otherwise, never refuse.

> “I NEVER ASKED FOR THAT”: no scope added by the coordinator.

The plan makes these operational: decide a stop when evidence arrives, before composing another attempt; two unit corrections at most; material must fall; a repeated class stops. Environment failure is never a unit round. A split carries the unresolved finding and its stop history; a second stop on that finding asks instead of splitting again. A clean read closes a unit.

The **updated design row**, including its 2026-10-07 refinement, governs design critique: continue while material remains and falls, at most four completed examinations; repeat of a fixed class in the same section stops; zero material accepts. On stopping, fold concrete changes into unit acceptance items and split only the remaining unbuildable sections. If splitting leaves no buildable unit, fold all concrete changes and accept that buildable design. Never ask a person to decide this convergence exit. This supersedes the older one-round/critical-second-round sentences in finding 20, the mechanisms page's design `ask` outcome, and `skills/design-critique/SKILL.md`. It does not replace a person's approval of a goal or their choice to hold the helm.

The parent plan promotes the design-critique practice now; its machinery implementation moves to the follow-up. This page does not claim that the plan's two-goal measurement condition was independently measured here. The follow-up retains the measure: material code-read findings traced to the design or brief per unit, compared with the plan's 1a baseline of about six out of thirty findings.

## What it delivers

One recorded answer says whether a review closes, permits a correction, transfers work or stops. Every reader uses the same findings and classes. Builds and unit proofs use the same declared cheap check and audits. A round owns its worktree and its resulting tree; publication reconciles a moved branch instead of asking for another build merely because its parent moved. Design-convergence implementation and drop effects belong to the follow-up below.

**Step 1 is unit `unit-stop`.** It holds the unit read record, the two dispositions (`split: UNIT` and `dropped: REASON`), the stop policy and “a green read closes the unit”. `work review G --work U` records the read and stop; `work revise` consumes that decision, so zero findings cannot buy another agent build and transferred work remains required for completion. A drop disposition is only a prepared proposal: an unrequired unit stops with an `ask`, retains its code and remains open. This goal implements neither automatic drop effects nor a person's removal of a required completion row. Existing person authority remains in place.

The other five units extend those owners to empty builds, worktree custody, publication and declared checks. No generic loop registry, scheduler, fleet database or new register service is built. The threat is mistakes, stale observations, concurrent commands and interrupted processes by trusted operators and agents; hostile tampering is outside this design.

## Decided by m1e for Wido

1. Use the existing `review.stop` policy (`auto`, a decimal correction cap including zero, or `person`). Do not add the plan's earlier three separate correction toggles. Strict falling material and class detection are invariants of automatic continuation.
2. Extend the lane's stop **value shape**, with one small pure decision function, and retain stores beside their subjects. Do not move the lane's append-only records onto main. The lane keeps its command wording and question synchronization; review chains supply their own remedies.
3. Extend the existing finding register and goal review obligations for transfers. A transferred finding keeps its original read-qualified identity; a new read gets new finding identities and explicitly refers to what it clears.
4. Keep production critique counts separate from transport executions. Freeze a unit's correction allowance; an environment retry never spends either or the goal's work allowance.
5. Retain `--check` as a person's explicit manual check for repairing missing declarations. An agent cannot replace the declared rung with it. The verb prints and records the impact before the exception takes effect, then records its check result; it never calls missing proof green.
6. The goal-2 design covers finding 6 in full. Roster audit, effort selection, Readers scaffolding and citation validation remain goal 6; provider coordination and general launch deadlines remain the fleet goal. The cheap-rung deadline belongs here because this goal executes it.

## Decisions, with sites

### 1. Record what the stop actually reads

**Current sites.** `internal/launch/unit_run.go:65` has a round material count and cause, but `roundFindings` at `:784` falls back to text verdict counts and relations. `internal/launch/record.go:59` has a measurement count without a structured read. The code critic schema under `internal/protocol/schemas/` requires id, severity, material, claim and evidence, but no class or location. `internal/returnschema/complete.go:581` already checks that the verdict count equals material rows. `cmd/metasystem/intent_work.go:883` assigns one output filename to a unit; `internal/launch/declared_outputs.go:44` locks and archives that shared path. `internal/dispatch/finding_register.go:46` owns registered findings.

Add a typed `Read` value beside `readsubject`, used by collection, dispatch and launch without a dependency on either runner. Its fields are the mechanism's: `id`, subject tree and optional commit, engine identity, resolved model, material count, findings, output path and optional `carriedFrom`. A read id is the actual launch/job examination id, not the unit or chain root. Each finding has `id = read-id:number`, class, severity, material, title/claim, evidence, `where` and concrete `change`; it may name a prior finding through `resolves` or the existing relation. Classes are exactly `regression`, `weakened-test`, `incomplete-item`, `false-premise`, `faked-seam`, `missing-reader`, `scope`, `other`. This class is distinct from the existing bounded/severe rigor classification.

The collector allocates outputs beneath that launch's own state directory before dispatch. The return and prose output are immutable after collection. The collector validates the count, records the executing engine's identity (not a newly sampled host version), and writes the same read value into launch and round records. Until the fleet owns a monotonic engine stamp, the executing engine's existing build identity is provenance only; it is never compared as a sortable fleet stamp. The read's canonical bytes and digest accompany a published attestation, so another checkout can read them without the original launch directory.

No counting from a prose verdict after this cutover. Missing findings, missing classes, a prose verdict inconsistent with the structured findings, an inconsistent count or unreadable evidence means unknown, never zero. Existing complete reads remain valid for their original clean attestation. For an in-flight old chain, collection upgrades from its retained structured return when all fields are present; otherwise it records unavailable stop inputs and holds agent continuation. It does not invent classes from wording or retrospectively revoke already landed work. Recover collection from retained evidence if possible; otherwise allow one fresh examination under the retry rule, without spending a completed unit attempt, correction or goal allowance. If its inputs are still unknown, the unit owner records `decision=stop`, `handoff=stopped <cause>` on the `unit-round` itself and its stop-register entry, holding the unit. The retry record alone does not discharge this handoff; no third examination or automatic revise starts. A person can still make an explicit act through existing authority without unreadable advisory evidence, with the applicable prior impact statement; no act fabricates a clean read.

For unit reviews, `where` is a repository-relative path, with no absolute path or parent traversal; line details belong in evidence. Class recurrence means a material class seen in an earlier completed read of this unit reappears at a different path, or a finding identifies the earlier unresolved rule. The same class at the same path without `resolves` counts as the earlier finding unresolved, not as a fresh finding; a `resolves` reference must be validated against that prior finding. Two findings of one class in the first read are not recurrence. `other` requires the reader to name the rule in its relation; it is not an escape from recurrence detection.

### 2. Decide at collection, apply at the existing review verb

**Current sites.** `internal/landing/plain/stop.go:18` defines the shipped stop, `:143` records proof stops and `:244` synchronizes their questions. Its close search groups by loop, appropriate to the single lane but insufficient for multiple unit subjects. `internal/launch/coded.go:43` and `:78` enforce caps/divergence when another attempt is requested. `internal/launch/unit_revise.go:359-365` checks each finding's decision but succeeds for an empty finding set. `cmd/metasystem/intent_review_binding.go:237` sends accepted material back to revise. `internal/validate/critiqueclosed.go:268` has the five old dispositions.

Extract the stop value and the cause value (`internal/landing/plain/cause.go:12`) into `internal/loopstop`, with `Decide` as its one pure function. The lane uses aliases and passes its existing red/retry facts; its JSON and behavior stay compatible. The first new caller is read collection for the unit review. No I/O or command rendering belongs in `Decide`. Its inputs are the loop's declaration, prior completed evidence, current evidence, and effective actor/policy; its output is the extended stop shape, including `stopped <cause>` for exhausted unknown unit inputs alongside existing unit, lane and retry handoffs; the design caller and its accept/split outcomes move to the follow-up. Each owner supplies the subject-specific arguments and performs the handoff.

Review decisions live in the existing run or critic root, atomically with the collected read; the key is `(loop, subject, attempt)`. A unit subject includes goal, unit and root run. A retry subject is the original failed launch, with its executions attached. Decision values are `close`, `continue`, `stop`; the lane's existing `repeat` is read as `continue` by the shared value reader. When the decision is `stop`, that owner also writes a register entry of kind `stop`, keyed by the same `(loop, subject, attempt)`, with the decision as its evidence and matching handoff/closure state; replay creates or joins that entry before applying the handoff. A close names its own subject, never every loop of the same name. Read, decision and handoff application carry stable operation ids so a lost response cannot produce another split or model launch.

Evaluate in this order:

| Evidence | Unit decision | Next act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed, fully checked read with zero material | close | Publish its read; next declared unit |
| First material read, correction allowance remains | continue | Await bound dispositions, then one correction |
| Later material read, count strictly lower, no repeated class, allowance remains | continue | Same |
| Count equal/rising, class repeated, or two corrections spent | stop | Split required work; otherwise ask carrying a prepared drop without applying it |
| Second stop of any transferred unresolved finding | stop | Ask for that finding; never transfer again |
| Unknown read input | stop | Recover collection or allow one fresh examination under the retry rule; if still unknown, `stopped <cause>` holds this unit; an explicit person act remains available |
| Unknown failure attribution | stop | Hold and ask with the cause under Decision 3; never infer `own` |
| Effective policy `person`, with work left to decide | stop | Prepare the same proposal and ask for the act |

The first build/read plus two corrections is three completed unit attempts. A numeric `review.stop=N` replaces the two corrections with N, under the goal's approved allowance; zero closes or hands off at the first material read. The allowance is fixed at unit admission; a current helm hold is re-read before the next autonomous effect and can stop it. Returning the helm does not erase history. A missing or malformed policy never becomes `auto`.

Collection writes the decision **before** preparing a revision brief. Dispositions explain and join every finding; they cannot rewrite the read's material count to manufacture progress. `work revise` and the older `UnitRunner.Advance` follow-up path both consume the decision. A clean or stopped unit admits no automatic correction. A person's `work revise ... --reason TEXT --by NAME` prints and records an impact statement with their reason before admitting the requested act; it does not reset autonomous counters. Rebuilding a clean unit autonomously is new work with a new name and brief.

`split: UNIT` and `dropped: REASON` join the existing dispositions grammar. `work review G --work U --dispositions FILE` applies them. In auto mode the stop prepares and applies the split when U is required by the accepted Units table, including work inherited by a previous split; for an extra unit the design does not require, a drop disposition remains a prepared proposal in the stop's ask. It applies no drop effect and excludes no required completion row. An unreadable design cannot justify a drop. A prepared drop covers the whole unit and all its unresolved findings, leaving its tree intact; otherwise transfer all unresolved material, allowing named destination units.

For every unit-round stop that asks, the unit review owner writes or joins one mechanism-12 ask record per unresolved finding, keyed by `(loop, subject, attempt, finding)`; the decision and stop-register entry retain their `(loop, subject, attempt)` key. Each ask's `needs` is one executable command with known arguments filled in, printed by `question show`: for a required unit eligible for its first transfer, `metasystem work review G --work U --dispositions FILE`, with the prepared file carrying `split: UNIT`; for an unrequired unit, one applicable person command is selected from `metasystem work revise ... --reason TEXT --by NAME`, `metasystem goal accept-risk G --finding F --review R --reason TEXT` or `metasystem goal done G --reason TEXT`. Each unrequired-unit ask also lists its acceptable acts and its closure rule: the steward closes it when any listed act's successful record names that finding and matches its subject, or when that unit closes. A required-unit ask likewise closes on its requested successful act record naming that finding and subject, or unit closure. A reasoned revision's admission record names the findings it addresses; closing their asks does not declare the unit clean. The second stop on a transferred finding still never transfers again: its ask names the applicable person act instead. `goal accept-risk` takes exactly one `--finding` (`cmd/metasystem/intent_planning.go:1494-1520`), so accepting one risk closes only that finding's ask; other unresolved findings retain their asks. Goal conclusion uses Decision 5's chain cleanup. A text answer, prepared disposition, no-op drop or failed command never closes an ask; replay reconciles a successfully recorded act without performing it again.

A person-authored `dropped:` is answered plainly: “Drop effects are not built yet; goal review-drops-and-design-convergence owns them,” followed by the applicable executable command above; it is never silently recorded as a proposal or reported as an applied drop. Agent-prepared drop proposals retain the tree and remain context for the ask, never its requested act.

Unit-stop must never derive permission from a board card; the stop owner and existing authority boundary decide, and the board only displays their recorded state.

The smallest transfer extends `ReviewObligation` (`internal/goal/file.go:178`) with target unit, original read/finding, stop reference and transferred-once marker. `DeferFindings` (`internal/goal/verbs.go:2411`) publishes it before the original chain closes. The named destination and its generated brief join the goal's required completion set. `goalProgress` (`cmd/metasystem/intent_delivery.go:2247`), `work land` and `goal done` consume that set, including after a clone. The original read closes as transferred, never as clean. The destination's clean code read must explicitly cover each inherited id; then the obligation owner discharges it. An unrelated clean read does not suffice. The existing `work review ... --finding F --test NAME` remains the explicit evidence route.

A transferred source row is satisfied **through its destinations**: every destination is built/read and every inherited finding is discharged. Completion does not also demand an impossible clean source read. The destination's review covers the source's complete retained change plus its correction, and its published attestation names that coverage. Branch read admission consumes this coverage relation too; changing only `goalProgress` would leave the old branch gate blocking. No source attestation is rewritten as LAND. Transfers are within the same goal, acyclic, and a destination cannot be the source; a second stop on an inherited finding asks as above. Automatic destination names derive from the source unit and stop id, reserved once; an existing unrelated name is never reused. Its brief contains the original requirement, all retained findings and decisions, the stop history and the source's declared size estimate.

### 3. Stop empty, oversized and failed builds honestly

**Current sites.** `internal/launch/unit_run.go:413` plans proof immediately after build; `:694` finishes a round; `:724` treats a failed proof as counting regardless of attribution. The branch commit path only discovers an empty result later (`cmd/metasystem/intent_unit_review.go:195`).

Immediately after the builder exits, freeze its diff. Empty means `stopped (gap)`, retaining its last message; launch neither proof nor review. More than twice the declared changed-line estimate holds before proof for a person's decision, recording measured and declared lines. Count additions plus deletions in the same scope as the estimate, including tests; never shrink the check to make it fit. The person's explicit `work review ... --reason TEXT --by NAME` prints and records the impact of accepting that measured size before it takes effect, then resumes the pending proof, not a new build. A gap revision binds to the retained plan and gap, not to a nonexistent read or commit.

Every failed step uses the shared cause vocabulary. Only demonstrated `own` consumes an attempt. A test exit alone is `unclassified`; compare the same declared failing command on the round's base once, or reuse exact matching baseline evidence. Red on both is base/main evidence, not a defect charged to the unit. A known flake gets its one repeat. A command that cannot run, a lost process or tree movement is `environment`; one retry of that step uses the retained inputs and a fresh output path, never another build. A second environment failure ends the retry with its cause even if the kind changed; the same cause twice also stops immediately. A deadline ends with a stop and no automatic retry, following the host mechanism's explicit deadline rule. Unknown attribution holds and asks; it never spends a correction or returns work as `own`.

The unit collector and launch reservation owner reconcile environment executions out of the goal's counted attempts and reserved work charge by launch id. Replaying collection cannot refund twice. Physical execution ids remain in history. A main red joins the existing incident owner only when the evidence actually names main's tree; red on a branch base alone is not a fabricated trunk incident.

### 4. A round owns one worktree and one result

**Current sites.** `internal/launch/unit_named.go:316` hashes worktree, goal and unit; its lock at `:419` lasts for a command, so another unit has a different lock. `internal/launch/unit_run.go:437` takes proof snapshots, but `:527` ends after the read without another snapshot. `internal/launch/unit_review.go:93` consumes the retained proof result; `cmd/metasystem/intent_unit_review.go:177` refuses a changed head rather than replaying the result.

Extend the runner's ownership with one durable reservation keyed by canonical worktree, in its state directory, and a short transition lock. Fields: worktree, owner run/round, phase, retained subject, admitted child ids. The reservation lasts through build, proof and review judgement, across command exits. The same round's proof, committed review and publication are authorized owner acts; the owner must not wait on itself. Another unit, rebase or unrelated publication returns waiting with the owner and `work wait run:RUN`. Locks are taken in tree, unit, run order; none is held while waiting for another actor or remote result. The entire reservation, checks at **every tree-writing entry**, fresh proof/read boundary comparisons and recovery coverage ship together in `tree-boundaries`. Its first consumers are unit admission and branch mutation. It requires no drop effect; the follow-up will use this owner.

Fresh run/launch custody proves a child ended before releasing ownership; process absence guessed from elapsed time cannot. Terminal close, completed split and explicit cancellation release only after every owned child has ended. A continue decision retains ownership for that unit's next correction. The machinery's state, output and logs stay outside delivered code paths. Human recovery uses the existing cancellation/ownership path to quiesce that exact owner; a person may request replacement despite unreadable advisory state, but no act claims that two writers safely own one live tree.

Record the actual result tree, its parent, patch digest and proof identity on each round when its build ends. Compare fresh bytes before and after proof and after a read, and again under the tree lock at publication. A changed tree is an environment cause, never a clean read. A snapshot retained before a command yielded is evidence of that moment, not a fresh observation on resume.

At publication, fetch the current branch tip once under the branch owner's usual access checks. If it differs, replay the round's frozen patch onto that tip in scratch. A conflict holds with the exact conflict and names a correction bound to the retained round; an unrelated tip move alone never requests a rebuild. Prove the replayed tree through the cheap rung before committing. Reuse the read only through the existing change/fold-equivalence checks; if the subject's meaning changed, read the replayed result. A correction commit passes its own static/cheap gate before it is installed, even if later units already compile on top. Record the commit operation before writing it and reconcile that operation after interruption, as the existing publication owner does.

**Use of the wait.** A blocked writer can prepare its brief and inspect immutable inputs without touching the owned tree. Changes to the design or branch invalidate that preparation. It starts no extra model or proof job; capacity and the owner's completion therefore cannot be delayed by speculative work.

### 5. Publish and carry the same read on both routes

**Current sites.** Unit and by-commit reads meet at `cmd/metasystem/intent_unit_review.go:636`. Promotion at `:409` checks model independence and exact parent. `internal/goal/branch/read.go:87` owns local publication state. `internal/goal/branch/read_publish.go:30` publishes idempotently and confirms the remote contains the attestation. `internal/goal/branch/rebase.go:263` already carries unchanged reads after a fresh gate. `internal/goal/branch/attest.go:550` checks carry before source-specific handling, and `:899` copies the original source. Both unit-read and critic-root sources already work here; the plan's historical missing-by-commit-carry defect is not assumed still present.

Add the canonical read to both publication bundles and carry it through that existing owner. A carry creates a new read identity and subject with `carriedFrom`; it preserves the original immutable source and model/engine provenance. It does not claim another model examination or count another round. Keep the existing patch, fold, source and predecessor validation. Changed work is unread; lost or corrupt predecessor evidence is unknown. Neither is relabelled clean.

Range handling classifies each read with its unit: still on the branch; replayed equivalently and re-keyed; already landed and outside the remaining range; or changed and needing a read. A read attached to a unit already integrated under a proven equivalent commit is omitted from the active range while its evidence remains retained. It cannot refuse unrelated remaining units. No match by filename or unit label alone. `work rebase` resolves current remote main before testing ancestry and reports the actual behind count when it does nothing (`internal/goal/branch/rebase.go:90` is the existing endpoint-bound owner).

Expose the existing branch commit owner as `work commit G --work U` for hand changes, supplying the unit trailer and the same subject/gate path; callers need no undocumented trailer. On a person's `goal done ... --reason`, print and record the impact of closing the remaining review chains with that reason before the conclusion takes effect, then close those chains after the goal act succeeds, never as clean reads. Agent conclusion still cannot bypass transferred work or existing completion obligations. This cleanup is idempotent and discoverable from the concluded goal if interrupted.

### 6. Builder and unit proof execute one declared check

**Current sites.** `cmd/metasystem/intent_work.go:806` reads caller `--check`; `:897` makes it the proof command; `:447` prepends adapter-selected test steps. The builder binding at `:1137` only describes a proof after building. `cmd/metasystem/intent_landing_prove.go:43` already reads a declaration from an exact committed tree using `config.CommittedContentLookup` (`internal/config/resolve.go:490`). `internal/config/defaults.go:79` declares only the full and cheap commands.

Reuse that committed-content reader through a small `resolveUnitCheck` function. At round admission it freezes `proof.cheap`, `proof.audits`, `proof.deadline`, their source tree, working directory and the execution environment in the retained unit plan. `proof.audits` is one shell command, normally a repository script when several audits are needed; an explicit `true` declares no audits. The deadline is a positive number of minutes per command, matching mechanism 5's proof entry of `launch.deadline`. There is no hidden suite or audit default. Extend `settings keys/show/check` and adoption through the existing configuration owner. Commit this repository's `proof.audits=go test ./cmd/metasystem -run '^TestAudit' -count=1` and `proof.deadline=15` (minutes) in `metasystem.conf`, including the shipped template, in the same unit as enforcement; adoption still clears repository-specific declarations and leaves an adopter's required values explicitly missing. The declaration controls what runs; a brief names it instead of composing a suite.

`test run --unit-run RUN` is the public way to execute that frozen check. This option selects the unit's retained commands instead of resolving a testing-contract selection afresh; an ordinary `test run` inside `proof.cheap` therefore does not recurse. The builder's composed brief calls it before returning; the runner invokes the same executor again to attest. It runs cheap and audits in the declared order, retaining each command's exit and output, and returns failure if either fails. It never pipes away an exit code. Both uses share the proof step's host environment, working directory, cache access and process-group deadline; a sandbox difference is an environment failure. The unit proof is the attestation, not the builder's statement that checks passed.

The check's declaration bytes are frozen before build. Candidate edits to the declarations are validated and tested as candidate content but take effect for the next round; a build cannot weaken its own check after admission. Replay onto another branch tip re-resolves the committed declarations there and proves the new subject under them. The unit plan records both observations, rather than claiming the old check proved the new tree. Remove adapter suite injection and agent-supplied replacement commands from this rung. Full suite ownership remains the lane and trunk timer.

Publication and carry gates use this same executor. Rebase carry at `internal/goal/branch/rebase.go:319` calls `readGate` (`cmd/metasystem/goal_branch.go:105`) without a unit run: add a subject-commit entry to the check executor that resolves committed `proof.cheap`, `proof.audits` and `proof.deadline` at that exact subject commit, freezes their bytes and execution environment, and allocates its own retained execution id. It executes on that subject tree and records both exits under that id; it neither invents a unit run nor borrows a preceding unit execution. `internal/goal/branch/attest.go:544` currently requires the literal gate kind `go-gate-fast`; new attestations instead name `unit-check`, its retained execution id, declaration/command digest and exact tree. Validation checks both command exits against that execution. Existing `go-gate-fast` attestations keep their original validation; a new or replayed subject cannot borrow one to avoid the declared check. This cutover belongs to declared-check, so it never leaves an extra hard-coded suite behind publication. If the declaration at the subject commit is missing or unreadable, carry appends that unit to `NeedsReview` and continues the rebase, using the per-unit fallback at `internal/goal/branch/rebase.go:312`; it does not propagate that declaration failure as a rebase error, fabricate a gate or carry the old read. Record why the unit needs a read. This fallback never refuses a person: the rebase completes with its review-needed result and the explicit manual check/read route remains available without the broken declaration.

Declared-check bypasses `internal/goal/branch/read.go:203-233`'s cached `GateRunID` for new subjects, so each new subject receives its own declared-check execution and cannot reuse an earlier gate observation.

At new build admission, a missing declaration refuses an **agent** before a model is launched and names the key. Its remedy is the person's explicit `work build G --work U --brief FILE --check COMMAND --reason TEXT --by NAME`, accepted without first reading the broken declaration or policy. That route prints and records the impact of the explicit `--check` repair before it takes effect, freezes and executes the supplied command, records who selected it and why, and allows a declaration-repair unit to reach the existing human delivery exception if needed. `settings set` is not offered as the repair: shipped help correctly says committed proof keys are changed through a goal. No automated retry loops on a missing key.

RULING R-143-m1e, the requirement that a person understands an override's impact first, applies to reasoned `work revise`, size acceptance, the `--check` repair and `goal done` closing review chains: each owning verb prints and records, before the effect, what it lets through, what risk remains, what changes for later work and how to undo it, in plain English; the same statement is retained with who, when, what and why, and does not introduce another confirmation gate.

“Never refuse a person” concerns policy and advisory evidence. An impossible filesystem write or a Git conflict is reported as a failed/pending effect, with preserved intent and bytes, never misreported as success. Person identity is proved by the existing authority boundary before advisory reads; `--by` text alone is not proof. No new authority system is introduced.
