## What it delivers

The steward advances admitted unit work without a person repeatedly invoking a verb: it collects finished children, starts the next authorized step, obtains a worker's finding decisions when needed, publishes a clean unit immediately, and consumes a completed-unit boundary to prepare the next unit or hand in the finished goal. Capacity waiting is durable pending work, visible through `work status`; a restart resumes the same operation. `seat.driver=person` prepares the exact next act and waits for the person. Neither a queue wait nor a repeated notification is a correction round.

**Step 1 is D1:** a capacity-refused `work build` retains its exact pending operation, shows why it waits and can be re-entered through the same command without losing work or reporting a failed build. Automatic waking follows in D3. D2 makes interruption and cancellation recoverable; D4 connects the existing review and publication owners; D5 consumes the fleet boundary. Each unit can be used before the next one exists. The threat model is concurrent commands, process failure, stale state and ordinary operator/agent mistakes, not containment of a hostile same-user process.

## State, failure and use of the wait

The unit record is the queue. A pending act is prepared, waiting, admitted, satisfied, cancelled or invalidated; reason and evidence distinguish them. A launch reservation and an executed command are different facts. Recording the operation precedes admission; an effect record precedes marking it satisfied. All effects keep their owners' locks and identity checks. The driver passes typed arguments to existing command/domain owners, never evals its displayed shell command. No global ordered queue or cross-seat scheduler is added.

For a build, Manager.Start alone samples/reserves goal 3's capacity. For a managed suite, the existing proof owner alone acquires resources, rechecks authority and starts the command; D1 reuses its existing queue duration and excludes acquire wait from the execution deadline; D3 never competes with a live proof waiter. Expired caller waits do not exhaust command deadlines. Launch queue intervals and existing suite queue durations remain separate from physical execution and completed-but-uncollected time; the process-cost owner consumes each operation's evidence once, with no duplicate suite timing record. Unknown timestamps or old evidence remain unknown. The process-interventions follow-up owns aggregate cost-report integration, not these producers.

Capacity has no execution-attempt budget: it is one pending request, observed on bounded ticks until capacity, cancellation or invalidation. Actual environment executions retain goal 2's one retry, deadline stop and second-failure handoff. Correction counts and class recurrence remain goal 2's decision. Repeated unchanged holds update neither round counts nor notification identities. I/O failure cannot turn an intent into success; status names the failed persistence/read and retains the previous state. A person can invoke the prepared act before broken advisory policy is read, through the existing direct-person proof; the impact is explained/recorded and physical failure is reported honestly. `--by` text, missing lineage, a remembered command and a stored actor label never establish person authority.

**Use of the wait.** While a launch or capacity slot is awaited, the steward collects already-ended work and the existing worker may prepare the next brief or dispositions from immutable evidence. Changed design, subject, claim, boundary or finding decisions invalidate that preparation. No speculative build, suite, merge or extra model call starts for this purpose; capacity waiting therefore says “nothing runs meanwhile; the required capacity is unavailable” when there is no already-admitted safe work. Preparation never holds the waited owner's locks, delays its decision or counts as proof.
