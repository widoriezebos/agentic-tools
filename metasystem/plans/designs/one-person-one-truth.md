## Problem

On 2026-10-13 every step of putting a goal back into the machinery was refused to the person, and every refusal came from one of two roots.

1. **Three notions of the person.** Landing verbs take the enrolled terminal (`lanePerson`). Goal verbs (`goal open`, `goal claim`) take `--by`, and call the enrolled pane a seat until `--by` is typed (R-93). Work verbs (`work land --whole`, `work review`) take the grant's lineage of the seat shell and call the enrolled pane "this agent session". Twenty minutes and four scripts per goal, and a rule of Wido's broken: humans are never denied a verb.
2. **Facts kept as files the machine never verifies.** A breach stop's record lives in the artifacts of whichever checkout recorded it; the person at another checkout "cannot prove the stop batch complete" and the hint is the same verb that just refused (292). A run from two days ago, its pid long dead, still owns a worktree; `work wait run:X` answers with its own hint (293, 295). Four goals the lane returned still count as held claims, so the session can claim nothing (294). The review verb runs a 22-minute static gate before noticing the machinery it would dispatch through is stopped (297).

## Decisions

**D1 One person, kept apart from ownership.** Two questions are answered separately, and today's verbs blur them.

- *Who is the person behind this invocation?* One resolver in `internal/humanauthority` answers it from the proof classes the goal library already admits (`internal/goal/approval.go`: direct enrolled terminal, signed-in browser session, verified channel answer) plus one it does not yet name at terminal entry points: a live grant for the seat's session, which names the grant's person and logs the act as today's power-of-attorney answers are. The resolver adds no class and drops none, including the act-specific ones a verb admits on its own today: `goal release G --reason TEXT --by NAME` from an unenrolled terminal keeps its terminal-proof fallback with the supplied attribution (`cmd/metasystem/intent_planning.go`, `goalsync_mutations.go`); every such act-specific class is listed by the resolver per act, so a verb's admission after U1 is a superset of its admission before U1. Every terminal verb that today reads `--by` or the lineage to decide whether a person is present (`internal/goal/verbs.go` open and approve, `cmd/metasystem/goal_branch.go`, `cmd/metasystem/intent_delivery.go` land and review) calls it; the browser and channel entry points keep their own proof as they are. **Settled for the first slice:** `--by NAME` is optional at a terminal; when given it must equal the resolver's person or the verb refuses ("--by does not match the enrolled person"); when absent the resolver's person is the attribution. The enrolled pane is never an agent session; the seat shell under a grant acts for the grant's person; with no person the verb refuses with the enroll remedy and R-93 (a seat opens only a blocking defect) applies.
- *Who holds this goal?* Ownership stays what it is (R-147-m1k): a goal held by a session or a machine is not written by another, grant or no grant; transfer uses the existing takeover transition. `goal claim G` under a grant remains the session's claim with the grant's person as attribution; the personal reservation (`personalClaim`, which bypasses dependency, approval and quota admission) is taken only from a directly enrolled terminal, as `cmd/metasystem/intent_planning.go` excludes it from grant attribution today. U1 changes who is recognised as present, never who may write.

**D2 Verified facts.** A fact the machine needs before it acts is verified, never read from a file it hopes is there.
- A run is (amended 2026-10-15: a run is releasable only when its recorded process identity is dead and no child or critic retains custody; otherwise the command names the surviving owner) alive iff its recorded identity (pid, start ticks) is alive and none of the custody `internal/launch/tree_reservation.go` already checks survives (children, the critic). `work wait run:X` on a run that is dead by that whole test says "run X ended at T without a record; released" and releases the tree binding and the worktree; on a run with a surviving child or critic it names the survivor. `work review` and `work build` do the same check before refusing.
- A breach stop publishes its record to the ledger when it is recorded (today it exists only in the stopping checkout's `artifacts/agents/goal-stops`, `internal/goal/stop.go`); the fence's completeness is read from that published record. For stops recorded before this lands, and whenever no record exists anywhere, the person's `goal resume` lifts the fence as the person's act and records that it did, naming the missing record. Decided 2026-10-14 08:12 (Wido's question 4, Astra agreed): before the lift a cheap check reads the published stop record, then any directly locatable original record and its retained jobs and proof attempts, and reports complete, survivors found, or unknown; it never searches every checkout, and a batch state or an unchanged branch tip proves nothing (the stop record tracks pending jobs and proof attempts separately, `internal/goal/stop.go`). The lift is recorded as an explicit human override with the person, the stopped authority, the missing path, the observations and the uncertainty; it manufactures no completeness and releases no worktree custody (`internal/launch/tree_reservation.go` keeps its children-and-critic checks); known survivors are shown at once and in health. The check informs the person's act and is never another refusal.
- A return by the landing lane releases the session's claim: the goal goes back to approved, its branch and return reason attached. A returned goal never blocks another claim.
- The hand-in's "clean read per unit" accepts the lane's own read records for the units a tip read covered (lane-housekeeping-2 U6 already names the units); a hand-built unit with no "last unit commit" lands with `--whole` as today.

**D3 No circular remedy, cheapest check first.** A refusal's remedy is a different verb or a different state than the one that refused, and names the fact or file it lacks (path, record id). The audit that today checks "every refusal names an enroll remedy" gains a row: the remedy's verb is not the refusing verb with the same arguments. Verbs that dispatch through the machinery check that the machinery is alive before any gate or build (the review verb's static gate ran 22 minutes for nothing). Two more instances (findings 299, 300): the headline names `landing resume` for an open engine incident and for unstable checks, and resume touches neither; the incident's remedy is `landing incident clear ID`, the unstable checks' is a proof of main, and the headline says so. Delivery (amendment 2026-10-15): the incident and unstable-check headline remedies are delivered by the-lane-says-what-it-does; asks-clear-on-their-effect owns whether their requested effects remain outstanding.

**D4 The lane's own records never stale its engine.** Two things are compared today and must be told apart before U5 is built: the state record's `rebuild: true` flag on an `engine.stamp` drift event (`internal/landing/state/state.go`, which says the record was rebuilt from a fresh observation) and the engine stamp itself (`internal/enginebuild`, which says what the running engine was built from). U5 changes only the second: the engine is current when its build stamp's source matches main's engine sources by the one pathclass classification named in U5 (not a list of directories), so a records-only push by the lane (a flake sighting, a goal release) never asks a person for a redeploy (findings 273, 282). U5's first step is a read of which comparison produced the redeploy question of 2026-10-13 12:36. U5 also closes an engine incident whose engine stamp is older than the engine `landing deploy` just started (finding 299: the hf12 incident outlived its fix and two deploys), and a records-only move of main earns proof credit only through the affected-check and reuse rules owned by a-records-change-runs-its-affected-checks (findings 300, 303, 320, 321); engine freshness grants none. Decided 2026-10-14 08:12 (question 3, Astra agreed): the four-hour clock stays while a flake incident is open; U5 skips only the periodic clock proof, when main's code tree (by the path classification in `internal/landing/plain/scope.go`, not a list of directories) is unchanged since the last full green and no flake incident is open in the durable incident registry; the hourly debt (`scopedProofDue`), the changed-input and the changed-environment proofs stay. `fullProofClock` advances only on the result of a trunk proof that ran in full (green, or red with `CountedFull`) and on qualifying full-proof pushes; neither an unrun proof nor a scoped trunk result advances it (lane-proof-finds-its-own-engine D2–D3; a-records-change-runs-its-affected-checks D1a).

## Threat model and stop rule

- Threat: D1 widens who may act. Mitigation: the predicate only unifies what each verb already accepts somewhere; the grant's log keeps every act in the person's name; a `--by` that contradicts the enrollment stays refused.
- Threat: D2's "person's resume lifts the fence" could hide a real incomplete stop. Mitigation: it records the missing record's path and the person; the steward's health lists fences lifted without a record.
- Threat: D4 could miss an engine-source change in a records directory. Mitigation: the pathclass classification names those files as behaviour; the freshness test includes one.
- Stop rule for the design critique: two Astra rounds with zero material findings, or a round whose only findings are wording.
- Stop rule per unit: one build and at most two corrections; a third red returns the unit to the design.
