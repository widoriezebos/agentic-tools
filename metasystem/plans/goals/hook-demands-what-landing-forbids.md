# hook-demands-what-landing-forbids

- State: queued
- Priority: 3
- Sequence: 7
- Risk: severity=2 novelty=2 exposure=3 accumulation=2 basis="severity 2: no data is at risk, but a seat is ordered to do what it cannot land, and the correction is lost at the next checkout; novelty 2: the seam is between two enforcers that each work alone; exposure 3: every seat meets the hook and the gate; accumulation 2: the same stale plans are re-reported every turn on every seat until someone who owns them happens to look"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: What: The end-of-turn check and the landing gate stop giving a session contradictory orders about plans it does not own. Why: the check tells a session to record in a plan why its work is blocked or finished; when that plan belongs to another goal, the landing gate refuses to commit the edit, so the correction lives only in the working copy, is lost on the next pull, stash or landing, and the session is told to redo it. Already done: the refusal is bounded and does not repeat for the same work, the landing refusal now says to land it from the owning goal, and 170 finished plans moved to records, so fewer stale plans trigger it. Left: the check still orders the edit instead of naming the owner. Pros: no more lost corrections and repeated wasted turns. Cons: routing to the owner depends on the owner acting; the alternative, a narrow landing class for plan corrections, widens what a session may commit.
- Origin: main
- Next step: Next: make the end-of-turn check, when it names a plan the session does not own, name the owning goal and seat instead of asking for the edit. Done when: a test with a stale plan owned by another goal shows a refusal that names that owner and gives no order to edit the plan.
