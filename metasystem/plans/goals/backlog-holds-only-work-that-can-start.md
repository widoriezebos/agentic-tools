# backlog-holds-only-work-that-can-start

- State: approved
- Priority: 1
- Sequence: 2
- Risk: severity=3 novelty=3 exposure=3 accumulation=3 basis="Changes what a goal, an approval and a park mean for every seat and for the person; a wrong model loses work or floods the queue, and it shapes every future goal"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: The number of waiting goals stays at a minimum, ideally zero, because a goal is work that can start now and everything else is kept somewhere else (Wido 2026-10-03: 'We should really get into the situation/habit/proper prioritisation that tries to keep the number of waiting goals TO A MINIMUM, IDEALLY 0 ... 14 goals waiting is an indicator of something really wrong'; 'how can a goal that is waiting be NOT approved? If they are still according to original intent THEY ARE APPROVED'). Measured 2026-10-03: 110 open goals, 7 underway, 24 ready, 65 unapproved (median 23 days; most approved once and unapproved in bulk to slow building down), 14 parked. Wanted: (1) approval means agreement with the intent and stays until the intent changes; what starts next is decided by scheduling (a Ready queue with a cap tied to the seats), and the budget comes from the estimate; nothing is unapproved to slow it down. (2) Work that cannot start now is not a goal: it is an idea on a plain list, a follow-up stub on the goal it follows (opened by the machinery when that goal lands), or a watch entry the steward's patterns own ('if X is seen, propose goal Y'). (3) Started work pauses only with an owner of its release: a question to the person with a default and a date, or a goal that is underway; sequencing behind work underway is shown under that work, not as waiting. (4) Opening a goal that cannot start now, or parking one without an owner of its release, is refused with the plain alternatives. (5) Waiting, Ready against its cap, the oldest Ready goal and goals opened against goals landed are measured, shown and watched; a triage the partner prepares and the person decides in one sitting keeps them there.
