# stop-batch-strands-a-resumable-goal

- State: queued
- Priority: 1
- Sequence: 20
- Risk: severity=3 novelty=2 exposure=2 accumulation=3 basis="severity 3: a goal with complete reviewed work becomes permanently unclaimable and no verb can recover it, so the work can only be rescued by rebuilding it under a new goal; novelty 2: the binding check and the stop batch already exist and the repair is to let one of them move, though which one is a real decision; exposure 2: any breach-stopped goal whose capability advances afterwards, on any machine, which is easy to do because an ordinary edit or approval advances it; accumulation 3: every such goal is wedged forever and its budget, claim and rank stay occupied, so the queue degrades permanently rather than recovering"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: A breach-stopped goal whose capability advances after the fence was recorded can never be resumed, and no verb can repair it. Proven on account-provenance on 2026-09-08, where three human attempts each hit a different door. Its fence was recorded 2026-09-07T21:17:15Z with reason ELAPSED_LIMIT at goal revision 19; its StopCapability is now Generation 21, Revision 21, Machine m1d, while the stop batch at artifacts/agents/goal-stops/stop-account-provenance-r19-f1.json still binds goalRevision 19, capabilityGeneration 19 and machine null. VerifyStopBatchComplete (internal/goal/stop.go:249-259) requires goal revision, capability generation, machine, claim epoch, fence epoch and reason all to equal the current capability, so goal resume refuses that the batch does not bind the exact stopped authority. goal set-budget refuses because the fence stands and only resume may reopen admission. job breach-stop at the current revision, which would write a batch that does bind, refuses because the existing batch contradicts the accepted fence. goal steal refuses because only resume may replace claim authority on a stopped goal, and goal recover reconciles only the invoking machine's journal. The three doors are locked against each other and the goal is unclaimable forever, with its budget, claim and rank still occupied. This is NOT goal breach-stop-wedges-seat, which is about a stopped claim holding the one-claim quota and whose DONE explicitly preserves the fence on resuming; this goal is about the fence being unliftable at all. DONE means a stopped goal can always be returned to its human's control: either a stale stop batch can be re-bound to the current capability by a named act, or resume accepts a fence whose batch is stale when the stop's reason and epoch are unchanged, or the capability is prevented from advancing while a fence stands so the batch can never go stale. Whichever is chosen, no path may let a stop be cleared without the human act that clears it today, and a fixture proves an unresumable goal from the account-provenance shape becomes resumable while an unauthorised clear still refuses. The evidence and the exact refusals are recorded in records/misc/account-provenance-resume-is-wedged.md.
- Origin: main
- Next step: Design first, on the design lane, because which of the three repairs to take is a real decision with different blast radii: re-binding a batch touches the authority record, relaxing resume touches the check that makes a stop meaningful, and freezing the capability touches every verb that edits a stopped goal. Read internal/goal/stop.go around VerifyStopBatchComplete and the resume mutation beside it, the breach-stop writer that refuses to replace a contradicting batch, and what advances StopCapability. Say explicitly what must remain impossible: clearing a stop without the human act that clears it today. Canary before battery: the smallest proof is one goal fixtured into the account-provenance shape, a stop batch bound to an older revision than the capability, where resume succeeds after the repair and an unauthorised clear still refuses, each a named test with a ceiling. Live evidence: records/misc/account-provenance-resume-is-wedged.md carries the three refusals verbatim; goal account-provenance is the wedged instance and its chain account-provenance-build1 is complete through round 4 with only a closing critic and a landing left, so fixing this recovers finished work rather than only preventing future loss. The design should say whether a stopped goal holds its machine claim slot at all: a stop halts work, it should not wedge the seat. m1d is the true second wedge, through account-provenance.
