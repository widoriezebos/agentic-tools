# capped-round-continues-instead-of-restarting

- State: queued
- Risk: severity=2 novelty=2 exposure=3 accumulation=2 basis="severity 2: no data is lost, because chain rounds accumulate in the worktree, but a delegate that restarts redoes or conflicts with work already on disk and the churn can undo a correct change; novelty 2: recording a capped round distinctly and deriving what it achieved are new, though the worktree accumulation and the round records already exist; exposure 3: every round long enough to reach its cap, on every machine, and every headless node that has no coordinator watching; accumulation 2: each missed continuation costs one attempt from a goal's box and produces a diff nobody intended, and the second restart is harder to untangle than the first"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: Wido's word 2026-09-08, watching an 85-minute build approach its 120-minute cap: 'The handling of a restart after a cap sounds like a metasystem standard thing; not something you should have to handle.' Today a round is killed by its record alone, startedAt plus capMin (scripts/agents/dispatch.sh:1198). Its accumulated changes survive, because chain rounds open the same worktree and rounds accumulate there rather than starting clean, but nothing carries that fact to the next round. The coordinator has to notice the cap hit, read the worktree diff, and hand-write continue-do-not-restart into the follow-up brief. A fresh delegate reading only the original brief can reasonably start over, redoing work already on disk or conflicting with it, and a headless node has no coordinator to notice at all, which is the case the fleet path has to survive. DONE means the continuation is the machinery's job rather than the coordinator's: a round that hits its cap is recorded distinctly from one that failed, refused or returned, so a reader and a script can tell the three apart, and its successor is told that it is a continuation after a cap and that the worktree already holds its predecessor's partial work. Wido's steer 2026-09-08 is that this is probably all it needs, because a delegate can read its own worktree; anything beyond telling it the fact has to earn its place in the design rather than being assumed here. A fixture proves it end to end: a round killed at its cap, then a follow-up that is told what happened, does not redo the prior changes, and completes.
- Origin: main
- Next step: Design decides the shape; this record deliberately does not. Wido's correction 2026-09-08: 'are you not overcomplicating things? A restart after a cap hit, the agents should just be told it was a restart after a cap hit?' The simple reading is probably right, because a delegate can read its own worktree: what it lacks is the fact that its predecessor was cut off at its cap and that the worktree holds partial work. Start from that and only add more if the design shows the fact alone is not enough. Read the cap enforcement in scripts/agents/dispatch.sh around line 1198, the cap authority in internal/dispatch/build.go:222-243, how a follow-up round composes its brief, and how the worktree is prepared for a follow-up versus a fresh round. Keep two things out of scope and say so: raising caps, which needs supervision re-armed with a higher ceiling and is a different problem, and any scheme that reconstructs a narrative of what the killed round was thinking. Canary before battery: the smallest proof is one round killed at a short fixture cap and one follow-up that is told it is a continuation and does not redo the prior changes, as a named test with a ceiling. Live evidence to draw the fixture from: job clbm-build1 under goal chain-landing-after-base-move-recertifies ran 85 minutes against a 120-minute cap on 2026-09-08 with 19 files already modified in its worktree.
