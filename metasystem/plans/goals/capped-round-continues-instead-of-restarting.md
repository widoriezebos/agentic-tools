# capped-round-continues-instead-of-restarting

- State: queued
- Risk: severity=2 novelty=2 exposure=3 accumulation=2 basis="severity 2: no data is lost, because chain rounds accumulate in the worktree, but a delegate that restarts redoes or conflicts with work already on disk and the churn can undo a correct change; novelty 2: recording a capped round distinctly and deriving what it achieved are new, though the worktree accumulation and the round records already exist; exposure 3: every round long enough to reach its cap, on every machine, and every headless node that has no coordinator watching; accumulation 2: each missed continuation costs one attempt from a goal's box and produces a diff nobody intended, and the second restart is harder to untangle than the first"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: Wido's word 2026-09-08, watching an 85-minute build approach its 120-minute cap: 'The handling of a restart after a cap sounds like a metasystem standard thing; not something you should have to handle.' Today a round is killed by its record alone, startedAt plus capMin (scripts/agents/dispatch.sh:1198). Its accumulated changes survive, because chain rounds open the same worktree and rounds accumulate there rather than starting clean, but nothing carries that fact to the next round. The coordinator has to notice the cap hit, read the worktree diff to learn what exists, and hand-write continue-do-not-restart into the follow-up brief. A fresh delegate reading only the original brief can reasonably start over, redoing work already on disk or conflicting with it, and a headless node has no coordinator to notice at all, which is the case the fleet path has to survive. DONE means the continuation is the machinery's job and not the coordinator's: a round that hits its cap is recorded distinctly from one that failed, refused or returned, so a reader and a script can tell the three apart; the follow-up round automatically receives the accumulated worktree state and a statement of what the capped round had already changed; its instruction says continue from that state rather than restart; and the dispatcher refuses to present a capped round's successor as a fresh start. A fixture proves it end to end: a round killed at its cap, then a follow-up that observes the prior changes, does not redo them, and completes. The hard part, which the design must settle rather than assume: the killed process cannot write its own account, because it is killed, so either the dispatcher derives that account from the worktree diff or rounds checkpoint progress as they go. Say which, and say what happens when the account cannot be derived, which must be to state the uncertainty rather than to imply a clean slate.
- Origin: main
- Next step: Design first, on the design lane. Read the cap enforcement in scripts/agents/dispatch.sh around line 1198 and the cap authority in internal/dispatch/build.go:222-243, the follow-up path and how it composes a round's brief, and how the worktree is prepared for a follow-up versus a fresh round. Decide between a dispatcher-derived account from the worktree diff and in-round checkpointing, and say why the other was rejected. Note a constraint from the same day: the dispatch cap must stay below the live watcher's attested ceiling, so raising a cap means re-arming supervision with a higher --max-cap; this goal is about continuing after a cap, not about raising caps, and it should say so to keep the two apart. Canary before battery: the smallest proof is one round killed at a short fixture cap and one follow-up that observes the prior worktree changes without redoing them, as a named test with a ceiling, and the refusal case where the account cannot be derived must fail for the reason named rather than merely not continuing. Live evidence to draw the fixture from: job clbm-build1 under goal chain-landing-after-base-move-recertifies ran 85 minutes against a 120-minute cap on 2026-09-08 with 19 files already modified in its worktree.
