# capped-round-continues-instead-of-restarting

- State: approved
- Priority: 1
- Sequence: 21
- Risk: severity=2 novelty=2 exposure=3 accumulation=2 basis="severity 2: no data is lost, because chain rounds accumulate in the worktree, but a delegate that restarts redoes or conflicts with work already on disk and the churn can undo a correct change; novelty 2: recording a capped round distinctly and deriving what it achieved are new, though the worktree accumulation and the round records already exist; exposure 3: every round long enough to reach its cap, on every machine, and every headless node that has no coordinator watching; accumulation 2: each missed continuation costs one attempt from a goal's box and produces a diff nobody intended, and the second restart is harder to untangle than the first"
- Tier: 3
- Intent: Wido's word 2026-09-08, watching an 85-minute build approach its 120-minute cap: 'The handling of a restart after a cap sounds like a metasystem standard thing; not something you should have to handle.' Today a round is killed by its record alone, startedAt plus capMin (scripts/agents/dispatch.sh:1198). Its accumulated changes survive, because chain rounds open the same worktree and rounds accumulate there rather than starting clean, but nothing carries that fact to the next round. The coordinator has to notice the cap hit, read the worktree diff, and hand-write continue-do-not-restart into the follow-up brief. A fresh delegate reading only the original brief can reasonably start over, redoing work already on disk or conflicting with it, and a headless node has no coordinator to notice at all, which is the case the fleet path has to survive. DONE means the continuation is the machinery's job rather than the coordinator's: a round that hits its cap is recorded distinctly from one that failed, refused or returned, so a reader and a script can tell the three apart, and its successor is told that it is a continuation after a cap and that the worktree already holds its predecessor's partial work. Wido's steer 2026-09-08 is that this is probably all it needs, because a delegate can read its own worktree; anything beyond telling it the fact has to earn its place in the design rather than being assumed here. A fixture proves it end to end: a round killed at its cap, then a follow-up that is told what happened, does not redo the prior changes, and completes. RUNTIME INDEPENDENCE (plan principle 0; architecture doctrine of 2026-08-16: correctness never depends on an accelerator): this holds for every runtime the roster can host today (claude, codex, devin) and for future adapters such as opencode; the mechanism lives in the Go engine, the ledger verbs and the adapter contract, never in one runtime's hook, harness, CLI or transcript format; a native facility may accelerate it through its adapter, and an agent on any runtime with no accelerator gets the same guarantee from the records and the verb alone; where the goal touches a runtime path, DONE is proven on at least two runtimes.
- Origin: main
- Next step: Design decides the shape; this record deliberately does not. Wido's correction 2026-09-08: 'are you not overcomplicating things? A restart after a cap hit, the agents should just be told it was a restart after a cap hit?' The simple reading is probably right, because a delegate can read its own worktree: what it lacks is the fact that its predecessor was cut off at its cap and that the worktree holds partial work. RESEARCH FIRST, on Wido's word the same day ('Add a research topic to the backlog item we created around cap restarts because I suspect this is just stupid'): find out whether the current refusal protects anything real before designing around it. scripts/agents/dispatch.sh:2227 refuses a follow-up unless the newest chain record is completed or failed with error protocol_error, and excludes timeout, pending, running and process-lost; no comment anywhere states why timeout is excluded, so the exclusion is asserted rather than argued. The strongest reason the coordinator could construct: a killed round wrote no return, so nothing attests what state it left and a file could be half written, and a follow-up normally builds on a round that declared its own changes. If that is the reason then the remedy does not follow from it, because the refusal sends the caller to a fresh dispatch and dispatch.sh:1552 refuses to reuse an existing worktree, so the fresh round starts clean and the unattested work is DISCARDED rather than attested; the middle path nobody wrote is to let the successor inherit the tree and require it to attest what it found. Answer three questions in the design and record the answers: (1) what the timeout exclusion actually protects, in terms of a failure someone has seen rather than a hypothetical; (2) whether a half-written file is a real risk given that the successor can re-run the predecessor's build and canaries before touching anything; (3) if inheriting is safe, whether the right change is to admit timeout as a follow-up status with an attestation requirement, or to let the fresh-dispatch path adopt a stranded worktree - prefer the smaller change. Read the cap enforcement in scripts/agents/dispatch.sh around line 1198, the cap authority in internal/dispatch/build.go:222-243, how a follow-up composes its brief, and how the worktree is prepared for a follow-up versus a fresh round (dispatch.sh:1548-1560). Out of scope, and say so: raising caps, which needs supervision re-armed with a higher ceiling, and any scheme that reconstructs what the killed round was thinking. Canary before battery: the smallest proof is one round killed at a short fixture cap and one successor told it is a continuation that does not redo the prior changes, as a named test with a ceiling.
