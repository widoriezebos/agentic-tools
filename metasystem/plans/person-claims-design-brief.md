# Person claims — design brief

Working Mode: Design. Follow-up: `person-claims`, split from `plans/designs/lane-drain-and-fresh-claims.md` by the person's round-3 stop-rule decision. Round 3 returned one material finding, down from three, but repeated the seat-revival class in Decision 4/U4. Decision 5/U5 moves with it because claim preparation consumes U4's adoption. The accepted parent retains only U1a drain, U1b helm integration and U3 engine inputs. This brief grants no implementation, launch or spending authority and does not open or change a ledger goal.

## Scope and first usable step

Design person reservations and all-matching-claim adoption together: after the first unit a person can reserve goals without a running session, the next ordinary session adopts every matching reservation without replacing ownership or accounting, and only approved, budgeted work can execute. Keep agent claim preparation as the next unit, using that adoption owner. Re-estimate the follow-up before implementation; the inherited estimates below are context, not an approved budget.

Use today's accepted-ledger reads and existing publication rechecks. A stale projection delays adoption until the next session start after the reservation becomes visible. Fresh reads and the session-start hook's deadline belong to `ledger-reads-are-fresh`; this brief adds no fresh-read prerequisite. The accepted parent's U3 owns engine-input comparison; reference that owner rather than reimplementing it. Claim areas consume the accepted `lane-policies-and-helm` design's area owner. More autonomous waiting goals remain with runs-advance; person reservations do not expand automatic quota.

## Round-3 finding 1 — executability survives adoption

**Disposition: split under the parent's design stop rule; correction required here.** The epoch-only exclusion in the inherited Decision 4 is insufficient: adopting an unapproved reservation changes its capability to a positive epoch without granting approval or budget. After that seat ends, the steward could revive the claim and launch a successor merely because its epoch is positive.

Key the seat-ladder exclusion on executability, not epoch. In `SeatWorldFrom`, `internal/steward/seat_ladder.go:75–77`, a seat-lineage claim with no current approval or budget remains held but is not due: `StepDue = false`, `Wait = "awaits a person's approval"`. Apply the same executability check in `holdsUnderSeatLineage`, `internal/steward/seat_start.go:466`, before treating a claim as executable owned work at the start path (`:404`). Missing or unreadable authority cannot become due work. Preserve the epoch-0 capability fence for execution, but do not use it as a substitute for approval and budget checks. Preserve existing landing-step gates as well.

**Notification decision.** Keep the seat's existing one-working-claim notification. Retain a non-executable reservation in `SeatWorld.Held` as non-due rather than deleting it from the world; `PlanSeat` can then report that the held working claim awaits a person's approval. Excluding it from executable owned work must not lose that notification through an early return in the seat-start path. The follow-up design must connect those readers through the existing eligibility owners, without a second reservation register.

**Required public proof.** With no unrelated ready work, reserve an unapproved goal, adopt it through ordinary `metasystem session start`, verify that its real positive epoch did not create approval or budget, end the seat, then drive a steward tick through `SeatWorldFrom`, `holdsUnderSeatLineage` and `PlanSeat`. Assert held/non-due state, the approval wait and one-working-claim notification, no `ActRevive`, no launcher call, no spending and unchanged ownership/accounting. Cover absent approval and absent budget. Retain the pre-adoption epoch-0 no-launch case. **Mutation:** exclude only epoch 0; the adopted positive-epoch case must fail.

## Round-3 note — unrelated ready work

`PlanSeat` (`internal/steward/seat_ladder.go:162–191` and its following ready-work branch) can start a seat for unrelated ready work when no held working claim blocks selection. Therefore a blanket assertion that any reservation implies no launcher call is insufficiently scoped: identify which goal caused selection. Keep unrelated ready work absent in the decisive no-launch fixture above. Add a case with unrelated ready work present: the chosen one-working-claim notification policy keeps a held, unapproved working reservation visible and prevents bypassing it to launch that work; without such a held working claim, existing ready-work selection remains available. An independently authorized ordinary start may adopt reservations but cannot make them executable without approval and budget.

## Lineage and areas — notes carried through round 2

- **Lineage:** use a holder's `OwnerLineage` only when the holder is proved live or its lineage is stable across processes; a recorded dead holder with only the process-specific fallback is not a stable seat identity (`internal/lease/lease.go:101–102`, `internal/lease/verbs.go:338`). Reserve under `launch.SeatOwnerLineage` in that case. Adoption checks the actual session lineage (`internal/up/up.go:822–825`); report every same-machine personal mismatch as pending, with the working `session start --lineage <reserved-lineage>` remedy for a hand-started session without an explicit lineage. A different explicit lineage needs a fresh matching session, never relabeling. Test the dead hand-started holder and follow the printed remedy without release/reclaim or a new accounting episode.
- **Areas:** pass the proved actor into the accepted area's `ClaimAreas` selection and publication/recovery checks. A person's established overlap is a warning recorded in claim history with person provenance; an agent's established overlap still sequences work. Preserve unknown-area warnings. Test a person's overlapping claim through publication and history; a mutation that refuses it must fail.

These are the lineage and area corrections already present in the round-2 design, originating in round-1 findings 2 and 3; neither disappears in this split.

## Five design questions

Answer each for every new function, record and act, including the restamp changes:

1. Who calls it in production?
2. How fresh is every state a decision reads?
3. Is the actor a person or an agent, with the helm reserved to a person and refusal of a person limited to preventing damage?
4. Can every refusal's remedy succeed when followed?
5. Does every unreadable input fail safe for the agent without vetoing a person's advisory override?

**Reservation.** Remove `Claim`'s unconditional person rejection; replace it with direct person-proof validation before any advisory override, including transaction recovery. Do not let a grant or `--by` text alone create personal ownership. Extend the ordinary `ClaimRecord` with optional `By` (rendered `by`); no separate personal claim record. Resolve its machine normally; use the lease holder's `OwnerLineage` only when that holder is proved live or the lineage is stable across processes (an explicit owner lineage distinct from the process-specific holder main id, including `launch.SeatOwnerLineage`). The recorded holder alone is not liveness proof (`internal/lease/verbs.go:338`); a missing lineage falls back to the holder main id at `internal/lease/lease.go:101–102`. For an absent or dead holder with only that process-specific lineage, reserve under `launch.SeatOwnerLineage`, not the dead process id; adoption still checks the actual session's lineage (`internal/up/up.go:822–825`). Terminal enrollment still proves the person but its derived lineage must not become the goal's owning lineage. Scope this change to claim construction, preserving other human verbs' identities.

Remove **only** the synthetic `else if classification.Class == lease.ClassHuman { req.ClaimEpoch = 1 }` at `goalsync_mutations.go:704–705`; retain the preceding real classified-epoch assignment. With a real owning session epoch, bind it; without one, bind epoch 0. Epoch is already stored in `StopCapability.ClaimEpoch`; do not duplicate it on the claim. Extend `bindClaim` and parse/validate/render together: zero is valid only for a person-originated claim with `By` and the ordinary machine/revision/lineage binding. Negative epochs, forged provenance and mismatched capabilities stay invalid. Epoch 0 reserves the goal and its 1b areas but grants no launch or stop-execution authority.

A named person's claim overrides advisory pin, sequencing and claim quota, recording the exact warnings in its claim history with person provenance. Change at-rest quota validation in the same unit so these recorded personal reservations remain valid after adoption; an automatic claim still counts the seat's existing reservations and cannot use their provenance to acquire another goal. Adoption is continuation of those reservations, not a new autonomous claim or quota expansion. Pass the verified actor to 1b's `ClaimAreas` at selection and publication/recovery checks: an established overlap on a person's claim becomes a warning recorded in the claim history with person provenance, while an agent's known overlap still sequences work. This goal adds that actor input and person-warning branch to 1b's agent-only predicates. Keep 1b's unknown-area warning behavior.

Ownership and approval are separate. A person may reserve a live unowned goal without execution approval; preserve any existing approval, budget and provenance, and do not synthesize a budget when absent. Validation must represent that reservation as valid but not executable. Work admission still requires genuine approval and budget after adoption. A claim does not edit a park or a blocker into an approval. A claim already held by another machine/lineage needs the existing explicit `--take-over --reason` path; a terminal's authority is no excuse to silently steal a writer's work. Existing release and conclusion remain available to the person without release/reclaim being the adoption protocol.

**Seat revival.** Epoch-0 reservations are excluded from revive. `SeatWorldFrom` excludes them from executable held work in both claimed and landing inputs; `holdsUnderSeatLineage` applies the same capability check before the owned-work branch. `PlanSeat` must never select a reservation for `ActRevive`. Keep it reserved in the ledger, quota and areas; this does not make it a ready unowned goal. A reservation spends nothing and launches nothing. The seat adopts it at its next ordinary start, with approval/budget checks still required for execution.

**Adoption, including F4.** Extend `restampStopCapabilityForUp` to iterate **all** sorted claimed goals from today's `Project(endpoint, false, now)` snapshot inside this shared callback, adopting every matching machine-and-lineage pair and reporting same-machine personal reservations under another lineage as pending. Its current early returns on a missing capability, an already-current capability, a successful restamp or a per-goal error must not hide a later match. Extend `StopCapabilityRestampResult` to contain per-goal outcomes and aggregate them in `stopCapabilityOutcome`: adopted/restamped, current, or still pending with cause. There is no new durable adoption register.

Use `goal.Restamp` for each change, never `bindClaim`, which starts a new episode and clears related bindings. `Restamp` accepts a legitimate epoch-0 personal claim and sets its capability to the **real authenticated holder's positive epoch**. Keep `ClaimEpochForRebind`'s holder, MAIN, positive-epoch checks and `ownPair`'s machine-and-lineage check inside publication. Preserve the claim's `By`, revision, accounting revision, episode fields, approval, budget, capability generation/revision and fence; only the capability epoch and ordinary restamp history change. Positive-to-positive restamping keeps today's behavior, including refusal to move backwards. Do not adopt handed-over or fenced claims as ordinary seat work.

Before each mutation revalidate the live holder; publication reloads the goal and its current ownership. A concurrent release, takeover or holder loss is reported for that goal, not overwritten. Already-current is success/no-op and iteration continues. A missing capability on a legacy or damaged claim is reported as pending; do not invent it and do not skip later valid claims. Individual failures do not roll back successful adoptions. If authority or transport is lost, stop further mutations and explicitly mark the remaining matching ids pending. Repeating `session start` retries only what remains; current claims cannot hide pending ones. A foreign machine or lineage adopts neither claim. Do not silently filter a same-machine personal reservation under a foreign lineage as the current callback does at `cmd/metasystem/up.go:135–137`: report its id, reserved lineage and actual session lineage as pending. For a hand-started session with no explicit lineage, print `metasystem session start --lineage <reserved-lineage>` and follow that command for the real session; the announcement can fill its absent owner lineage (`internal/lease/verbs.go:151–160`) and normal holder/pair checks then govern adoption. If the session already has a different explicit lineage, name a fresh session started with that reserved lineage; never relabel its existing announcement or claim. The remedy resumes the reservation without a release/reclaim or a new accounting episode.

**Work remedy.** At the shared goal-binding reader, reject epoch 0 before generic stale-epoch handling, including when the caller has no current epoch. Print **`metasystem session start`**. After that act, reread the accepted binding; both approved reservations must be eligible under their unchanged approvals. If adoption failed, report its specific pending ids and cause; do not claim the session's goals are ready merely because the session is running. An unapproved reservation instead names the existing person approval act, preserving the rule that ownership spends nothing.

## Decision 5: an agent's claim prepares its real session once (U5)

**Sites.** `cmd/metasystem/intent_planning.go:1246` handles the actor late in `claimGoal`; `cmd/metasystem/intent_process.go:665` owns session start. `internal/up/up.go:713` enters engine/session preparation and `:803` distinguishes holder from advisor before restamping.

Move actor proof/classification before claim selection. Extract the existing session-start operation into one command-layer helper, `prepareClaimSession`, shared by session start and claim; keep the lifecycle in `up`, not in a new service. A person's claim goes directly through U4. An agent with an authenticated live owning session reuses it. Otherwise invoke preparation once in process for the original caller process and actual owning lineage, not a subprocess that becomes its own caller. U3 permits unchanged engine inputs and U4 adopts visible existing personal reservations using today's reads. Session preparation never recursively claims.

After preparation, reclassify the original caller, reread its holder/epoch and reload the decision with today's accepted-ledger read before selecting or publishing. A foreign live holder is not displaced; an advisor does not become a writer because preparation returned successfully. Failure publishes no claim and prints the underlying clearing act. Preserve the existing session-isolate remedy for a genuine concurrent writer; engine differences take the build/start route; publication transport problems retain their existing cause and retry remedy. A person's pause, drain, helm or system stop remains intact. A helm neither changes the claimant's actor class nor turns an automatic candidate into a person's choice.

**Five answers.** Public session start and agent claim call the shared helper; no other launcher is added. Authority is read before and after preparation, the final selection reloads today's accepted projection without a new freshness guarantee, and publication still rechecks. Only the real agent session acquires its own writer lease; person claims bypass preparation, never through a fake process. The original `goal claim` now succeeds after automatic preparation instead of sending the user around a claim/start cycle; a busy foreign holder's remedy must produce an isolated writer before retry. Unreadable actor, lease, executable or accepted ledger stops automatic ownership with its underlying cause; unreadable advisory policy cannot veto a person's U4 reservation. Preparation can have succeeded while publication fails; report that partial state and safely reuse the session on retry.
