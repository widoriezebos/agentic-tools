## Binding intent and scope

Inherit Wido's “No HAL 9000” and “YOU ONLY INTEGRATE WHEN YOU ARE DONE WITH A GOAL” unchanged: a proved person's act overrides advisory holds, while data safety and ownership remain protected; the helm belongs to a person.

- Drain closes automatic admission, finishes admitted work, then holds; immediate stop remains a separate pause.
- Preserve the original U6 intent: every reader that decides from the ledger (`goal claim`, `goal next`, `session start`) fetches the accepted ledger itself before deciding; re-arm compares engine code inputs only, so ledger, records and settings-only moves do not defer session start; a person's claim needs no live session, and an agent's claim prepares the session it needs.
- The records ref remains a later decision informed by these readers' diagnostics.
- The parent implements policies and a helm that sets the scope's policies to `person` only; any later helm/drain integration is designed here, with no dependency from the parent on this work.

Name a useful step 1, its owners, its public test and what it defers before starting this design's own critique loop; declare its stop rule and budget at that loop's start, without carrying implementation estimates over as spending authority.

## Five design questions

Answer each for every new function, record and act, including the restamp changes:

1. Who calls it in production?
2. How fresh is every state a decision reads?
3. Is the actor a person or an agent, with the helm reserved to a person and refusal of a person limited to preventing damage?
4. Can every refusal's remedy succeed when followed?
5. Does every unreadable input fail safe for the agent without vetoing a person's advisory override?

## Decision 3: drain closes admission under the queue lock (U3)

**Sites.** Extend the owner of `plain.HandIn` and its lock (`internal/landing/plain/queue.go:112`, `:262`), the keeper's waiting reader (`cmd/metasystem/landing_agent.go:249`) and shared status (`internal/landing/plain/status.go:114`). Immediate pause remains owned by `lane.ReadPause` (`internal/landing/lane/pause.go:35`) and `stopLane` (`cmd/metasystem/intent_landing.go:951`). Start currently clears pause only after readiness checks (`:719`).

New `plain.SetDrain`, `ReadDrain` and `ClearDrain` own one lane-local `drain` record beside the queue: `by`, `at`, `reason`, `source` (`person` or helm identity), `state` (`draining` or `held`). `landing drain` sets it under the same lock as hand-in. There is no captured duplicate queue: after closure the existing waiting lines are precisely the drain set. A same-goal/same-commit repeat or metadata update is idempotent and allowed. A new goal, newer commit, returned goal handed in again, or records hand-in adds work and is refused for agents. The agent's refusal names who drained it and waiting through `metasystem landing status --repo <lane>` or the open question; it never names `landing start` as the agent's remedy. Retrying hand-in alone cannot clear it.

A person's explicit hand-in may extend the drain set with a warning and provenance; the lane remains draining. This is the same human override as the policies, not an agent `--force` escape. The meaning “admits nothing new” applies to automatic admission. A hand-in racing drain is either fully before closure or refused, never half admitted.

The keeper continues existing waiting work while draining. It moves the record to `held` only when no waiting line, active batch, proof or regeneration remains. A returned line is no longer waiting; its later re-hand-in is new admission. An incident-held waiting line keeps “draining, N waiting” and the incident's action. A drain, whether draining or held, and a lane helm both suppress the periodic trunk timer. Empty drains do not launch barren agents; an incident proof necessary to unblock the admitted queue remains permitted by its proof policy. A drain taken during a proof lets it finish and the proven batch push. `landing stop` still wins immediately over automatic drain progress and ends regeneration as today.

`landing start` clears drain (readable or corrupt) and pause at a person's request before reporting independent readiness problems; a broken keeper cannot prevent clearing admission. It never returns a helm. The drain record is the only admission fence: clearing it reopens admission even while the helm stands, with no helm-scoped reopening record. A later new helm take writes a drain again. `landing start` remains audience both (`cmd/metasystem/intent_landing.go:192–201`): an agent may clear only a pause; while a person's drain stands, its start refuses naming that drain, leaves it unchanged, and points to status or the open question. `landing status` says “draining, N waiting”, “drained; holding”, or “paused; drain still stands”, and lists an unreadable record as unknown, never as empty.

**Five answers.** Callers are drain/start, hand-in, keeper and status; the queue lock serializes drain and enqueue, and keeper completion rereads running state and pending membership under that lock. Drain is a human control act; start is available to both actors, but only a person clears a drain, and an agent can only clear a pause or advance admitted drain work. A person's start command actually removes even an unreadable drain; it is not gated by that drain or a dead runner. Corrupt drain fails closed for an agent; a person can replace it with drain or clear it with start without first reading it. Status returns partial information on queue failure; it cannot claim zero waiting. A filesystem write failure is reported as a failed operation, preserving the previous fence.

## Decision 6: decision readers fetch; non-code movement does not stale the engine (U6)

**Sites.** The three public edges are claim (`cmd/metasystem/intent_planning.go:1084`), next (`cmd/metasystem/goal.go:428`) and session start (`cmd/metasystem/intent_process.go:665`). The existing projection supports bounded fetching (`internal/goal/project.go:126`, `:178`); the optional-fetch command reader is `cmd/metasystem/intent_goals.go:27`. Session start enters `up` before starting its session (`internal/up/up.go:713`). Re-arm compares whole commits at `internal/steward/runner.go:724`; source ancestry/skew verification is `internal/steward/rearm_resolver.go:519`, and projection comparison is `:632`. Human request construction and binding are `cmd/metasystem/goalsync_mutations.go:689` and `internal/goal/verbs.go:457`.

**Fresh decisions.** New `goal.FreshProjection` composes `FetchAdvanceBounded` (`internal/goal/fetchbounded.go:24`) with `Project` over the resulting accepted tip. Use the transport bound that kills and cleans up the fetch, not only the outer timer in `project.go:178`, which can return while its goroutine still runs. Keep accepted-ledger validation and the existing configured fetch budget. Claim calls it before selecting a candidate or returning a quota result; next before ranking; session start before any goal-based admission/selection. Internal callers receive that immutable projection rather than immediately reading the old accepted cache again. Display-only reads may retain their explicit fetch option. A local-mode ledger reads the current local accepted tip and performs no network operation. Claim publication still fetches and reevaluates in its transaction; a preflight fetch is not a substitute for that race check.

Fetch failure/timeout is unknown freshness for an agent: no new claim or automatic goal selection, one environment retry under the existing stop allowance, then a hold with the fetch cause. A person's read can display the last accepted view labeled stale. A person's explicit claim proceeds to the normal publication transaction, which must fetch/compare-and-swap before changing shared ownership; failure there reports inability to publish safely, never pretends to have claimed offline. A person's session start can prepare the session while reporting unavailable backlog and disabling automatic selection until a fresh fetch succeeds. Corruption or foreign-ledger identity cannot be ignored because doing so could overwrite somebody else's ownership.

**Code identity.** Replace the exact-commit deferral with the existing source-verification path, using a shared engine-input comparison in `internal/steward`. New `EngineInputsEqual` is called by re-arm and source/skew validation and supplies the same input identity to the dispatch preflight. It compares the committed files that build the engine: Go source, embedded assets and declarations, module/toolchain inputs and build scripts. Ledger/record files and runtime `metasystem.conf` settings are excluded; a compiled default in Go or an embedded settings schema remains code. Derive this set from the existing engine projection/build inputs, extending the current `internal,cmd` path list where it misses module/build inputs. Do not reuse the landing proof's ledger allowlist or exclude all configuration-looking files by suffix.

Resolve the stamped source and checkout commit afresh, verify the stamped source belongs to the configured landed history, then compare their engine inputs. Equal inputs allow the existing enrolled engine despite different whole commit IDs and clear the stale deferred note. Unequal inputs retain the existing rebuild/re-arm route. Unreadable stamp, missing source object or failed input comparison is unknown, never equality. Record source commit, checkout commit, input comparison and fetch observation in the existing re-arm/command diagnostic result. These measurements are the evidence for a later records-ref decision; there is no new ref or timer here. Dirty engine inputs and another live operation still retain their current protection.

**Person and agent claims.** Prove the actor before session preparation. Remove `Claim`'s unconditional human rejection. A person's claim binds to the seat's machine and owning lineage, using the ordinary claim record with a new `by` field and claim epoch 0. There is no separate personal-claim shape, fake process or live-session requirement. Resolve the seat's owning lineage from the proved pane; `by` comes only from person proof, round-trips in the goal record, and is shown in status. Remove the human request's synthetic epoch 1 for this claim path (`cmd/metasystem/goalsync_mutations.go:701–706`). Extend `bindClaim` and validation to permit epoch 0 only with proved `by`; the ordinary machine/lineage binding remains required. Epoch 0 reserves the goal and its areas but grants no agent execution authority. The seat's next `session start` adopts that same claim through the existing claim binder by binding the real owning session's epoch, preserving `by`, approval, budget and ownership episode. `ownPair`, work admission, board/presence and recovery use this binding; work admission accepts after adoption and refuses epoch 0 beforehand. A different machine or lineage cannot adopt it. A person can still release or conclude their own claim through the existing human verbs; no release/reclaim assignment protocol is added.

A person's claim records advisory overrides for pin, sequencing and claim quota on its own claim/history so at-rest validation does not veto the act after the command allowed it. Preserve the approval's budget and provenance; no budget is raised or approval forged by claiming. If the goal lacks execution approval, personal ownership is still recorded but cannot launch paid agent work until approval exists. Structural validation checks the recorded person provenance and permits epoch 0 only with `by`; machine/session quota remains strict for autonomous agent claims. Naming a goal already owned by somebody else still requires the existing explicit `--take-over --reason`, because silently stealing ownership risks concurrent writes.

For an agent with no live owning session, claim invokes the existing session-start owner once in process, for the real caller and lineage, then rereads the resulting authority and fresh projection before publication. Session start must not recursively claim. An existing live session is reused; a busy foreign holder is not displaced. Failed preparation publishes no claim and returns its actual clearing act; it cannot loop between “claim needs session” and “session needs claim”. The claimant's actor never changes because a helm stands. No new daemon or per-claim launch service.

**Five answers.** Production callers of `FreshProjection` are the three decision edges, and the agent preparation path; re-arm/source verification call `EngineInputsEqual`; claim/parse/render/validation and ownership readers consume `ClaimRecord.by`, and session start binds the seat's epoch-0 claim to its real epoch. Freshness is one successful fetch per decision snapshot, with the claim transaction's own reread and current authority after preparation; source comparisons name immutable commits and do not cache an answer across changed input identities. A proved person bypasses advisory admission, an agent prepares only its actual session. The same printed `goal claim` succeeds after its automatically prepared session exists; a real engine difference names the existing system rebuild/start remedy, while ledger/settings-only movement no longer needs it. Unreadable freshness or engine inputs stop autonomous admission; unknown areas follow U5's warning path. Unknown actor/source or ledger damage never becomes authority or a safe publication by default.
