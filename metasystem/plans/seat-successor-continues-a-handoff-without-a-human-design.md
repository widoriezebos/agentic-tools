## 2. The shape

The seat over the trigger runs one verb, `metasystem context handoff --root <installation> --note <path>`, which ends its waits, records the state, stages the continuation, launches it through the revive seam that exists, and returns only when the successor's job is running with a probed pid, within 60 seconds. The successor is a headless steward continuation in the delegate class for its whole life, running under its own lineage, its job id; it never announces as a main and the lease never moves. Its first act, `context resume`, verifies the state, takes the claim by one authenticated leg that rewrites the claimant pair, records dead time, and prints one restore command per inherited wait; each restore is a live `wait --restore` process under the continuation's custody, and its Stop is blocked until every one runs. The predecessor is fenced from every goal mutation by its runtime-session token, checked again inside every ledger transaction, and each of its Stops is allowed with one console line naming the successor's status. The continuation runs the seat's own hooks, byte for byte, and hands off the same way at its own trigger; it ends only when its successor is confirmed, when it is superseded, or when the backlog is empty. Every attempt has its own job id and authorization; the burst retries stop at the cap, the tick retries once per 600 s until the handoff is 8 hours old, then the handoff expires with a notice and the claim goes back to a live predecessor or is released. A dead successor's claim stays with the chain's last real pair until the replacement's leg takes it; nothing synthetic ever holds a claim. Deny mode ships last, behind Wido's word, with a reserve of R calls past the trigger.

## 3. Decisions

### 3.1 Authority and class (SR2; CC8D-201; SSTB-303) — unchanged from revision 2

- 3.1a The successor is a steward continuation in the delegate class for the whole life of its predecessor and after it: it never announces as a seat main, lease/claim.go:117-118 gains no exception (Q4: no), and main-ness returns to whoever announces after the predecessor has ended, as today. Its goal authority is the claim pair moved by 3.7; its wait authority is custody (3.6).
- 3.1b Its own handoff is the delegate-class record that exists (context_verbs.go:223-233; handoff_capture.go:282-309); `EndInFlightWaits` selects the caller's rows by class `run.WaiterStateInFlight` and the caller's `MainId`, which for a continuation is its job id (3.6a).
- 3.1c (new, SSTB-303's pair) The successor's lineage is its job id. The launch seam (3.3b) exports `METASYSTEM_OWNER_LINEAGE=<jobId>` beside `METASYSTEM_DELEGATE_ROOT` for the `delegate --revive` process, the export goal mutations read (goalsync_mutations.go:472-476); `Custody.Lineage` (3.6a) is the current attempt's job id by definition, never read from the environment. So the claim pair (machine, `steward-<nonce>-a<n>`) is distinct from the predecessor's (machine, seat lineage) and from every other job's; `ownPair` admits exactly the process that exported it. That dispatch passes the export through to the provider child is checked by the builder (section 8); the witness reads the seam's environment and the custody.

### 3.12 Runtime independence; cancel and expiry

- 3.12a Launch, resume, the leg, the fence, the marker, the reaper, the slow retry, the expiry and restoration are files and verbs with no runtime check; the continuation's runtime and model are the roster's as recorded in the intent (stage.go:129). The per-adapter parts are 3.4a's copied hook settings and the gate's delivery, designed for Claude here; another runtime's adapter must install equivalents, and until it does a non-Claude continuation runs with no Stop gate and no tool gate. That gap is stated, and its witness is row 3.12a.
- 3.12b Cancel and expiry (R2-01; R-116-m1e item 5). A live held intent is cancelled by `--cancel` through `CancelIntent` as today and by the cap group's expiry unit through `handoffExpiryRule` at `RecordedAt + HandoffExpirySeconds`; that unit names its notice owner. A consumed intent is cancelled in place by `cancelConsumedHandoffLocked(root, cfg, nonce, reason)`: admitted while the marker is `held`, `launching`, `failed`, `recovering` or `missing` (never `running`: a running successor owns the work; `HANDOFF_CANCEL_SUCCESSOR_RUNNING`); in order, (1) 3.7d's return when the predecessor is `Alive`, else 3.7e's release, (2) `CancelledAt: now`, `CancelReason` (`cancelled by <caller>` or `expired`) through `UpdateConsumedIntent`, (3) for an expiry, `QueueNotification{Nonce: <nonce>-expired, Message: steward: handoff <nonce> expired after 8h with no running successor after <k> attempts; claim <returned to <lineage>|released>; record again}`, delivered by `RunTick`'s notice path (tick.go:215, :255), the named owner; `--cancel` prints its result and queues no notice. The expiry is called by `RunTick` (3.3i) and by the reaper when a job closes at or after the instant. A cancelled intent has marker `cancelled`: the gate re-blocks the predecessor with the record-again sentence, the fence lifts, `ConsumedActive` excludes it, the reaper and the slow retry ignore it, `--retry` refuses `HANDOFF_EXPIRED` or `HANDOFF_CANCELLED`, and `CompleteRevival`'s `CancelIntent` (revive.go:143) queues no notice of its own. A crash between (1) and (2) re-runs (1) idempotently on the ledger's state; between (2) and (3) repeats the queue write (same nonce, an overwrite), the reaper's own pattern (reap.go:44-58).
