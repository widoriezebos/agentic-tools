# What the chapter tells (the facts; use none of these labels verbatim)

On the third of September, one machine built, in three landings across
a day and a night, a single answer to "what may change this file, and
how". Before it, three hand-kept lists disagreed at the edges and every
gate read a different one; most landings of the previous day carried a
would-refuse stamp because the floor confused the cycle's paper trail
with behaviour. The answer is one committed manifest with four
classes: behaviour, which changes only through a reviewed chain;
records, the paper trail and the registers, landed by the seat that
owns the stream; the ledger, the goal files, changed only through the
goal verbs; and runtime, never landed at all. A path with no class is
refused, naming the manifest, so a new kind of file forces one
decision in one place. The first landing put the manifest and the verb
that answers from it in place and deleted the hand lists. The second
taught the landing evaluator to decide by class, to refuse the exact
revert of a record, to bind a record to the goal whose holder lands
it, and to stamp every landing with its goal. The third promoted the
last observed refusal: a carriage landing that touches behaviour is
refused, while records under a held goal pass. The work moved
machines halfway: the first machine handed over with its reviewed
tree pushed as a branch, and the second machine re-issued it,
reviewed it again, folded one wrong fixture expectation, and landed.
What it taught: a fixture can encode the very defect a fix removed;
records must not depend on the machine that wrote them; and the cost
of a gate is paid by whoever lands last.
