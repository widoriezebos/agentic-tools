## 0. The contract this revision builds to

Wido's reading of the DONE sentence (2026-09-13): the bound is by construction where the engine owns the surface, and by instruction and measurement where the harness owns it, with the week of section 7 failing the goal on any recorded call over the ceiling. The engine owns its verbs' output, the packets it composes, the continuation it stages and the handoff it records; a hosted session's own file reads and provider invocations belong to the harness on every runtime the roster has, and no engine seam stands in front of them. The words below say which of the two each mechanism is.

## 1. What the measurements say

On m1e on 2026-09-12, `metasystem goal list` printed 19.3 MB of JSON for 645 goals: approved 11, claimed 3, done 311, open 167, parked 4, queued 149. `History`, containing every ledger entry for every goal, occupied 18.3 MB; `NextStep` occupied 0.58 MB and `Intent` 0.51 MB. These are rounded byte measurements supplied for this design. They supersede the goal page's earlier 9 MB observation. `goal show --id` printed 32 KB; `supervise status` printed 196 bytes.

Across today's three seats' Claude transcripts, a fix-forward day with no delegate rounds, tool results totalled 6.7 MB. The seats' own reads dominated: `sed` 1.34 MB over 495 calls, `grep` 0.65 MB, and `Read` 0.42 MB, with single reads reaching 58 KB. Engine output was small: one `goal show` result was 30 KB and one `goal claim` result 29 KB. Nineteen `goal list` calls contributed only 0.07 MB because the seats already piped them through `jq`. The unfiltered 19.3 MB measurement and the filtered transcript measurement describe different surfaces.

The audit days had a different mix: the coordinator loop repeatedly read `goal list`, watch results, test logs, and landing output, while the conversation accumulated toward the million-token ceiling. `artifacts/reports/delivery-deep-dive-2026-09-11/claude-sessions.md` section 1a reports coordinator means of 386 to 507 thousand prompt tokens per call, versus 76 to 126 thousand for delegates. Section 4b records automatic compactions around 967 thousand tokens, each dropping about a million, roughly one per active seat per day. Its exact count is 19 compactions across the cohort, including manual compactions at smaller sizes; the daily description is approximate.

That audit file is retained local evidence in the m1e source checkout, outside tracked source. The 2026-09-12 measurements above were taken on m1e by the seat that owns this page (goal list piped through a byte count, the three seats' transcripts summed by tool result). Both belong in the retained baseline for the implementation proof.

The decision follows both observations: remove accidental bulk at the verb, then bound conversation accumulation across all reads. A small status result is no reason to remove it; a shell filter is no protection against the next unfiltered invocation. Smaller individual responses alone cannot prevent hundreds of useful file reads from accumulating.

## 2. Bound output where each verb produces it (by construction)

Slice 1 landed the measured offender: `goal list` prints one line per live goal in rank order under 64 KB (27 KB for 167 goals on 2026-09-12), `--json` the records without history, `--json --history` the old bytes, `--done` the archived bucket; `goal show` prints the page without history unless `--history`; a record flag on the summary is refused, never ignored (cmd/metasystem/goal.go, cmd/metasystem/goal_list.go).

Slice 1b adds the spill contract for the producers measured over the bound, and only those. The inventory, from the 2026-09-12 transcripts and the verbs' own code: `publishTestingResult` in its no-path mode (cmd/metasystem/test.go prints the whole TestResult with every group, 30 KB on that day; the landing path passes `--result` and is untouched), and the landing script's failed-step output (scripts/agents/land.sh prints forty lines of a failed step's tail and every coverage-delta failure in full, from a temporary combined step log it deletes on EXIT). Retracted from revision 2: `goal claim`, `goal next` and the other ledger mutations print only `outcome`, `tip` and `detail` (`printSyncResult`); the 29 KB measured on 2026-09-12 was a `goal show` read through the claim's follow-up, already bounded by slice 1.

- Owner: `internal/output`, `MaxInlineBytes = 32 * 1024`, one emission at a time (a batch of progress lines is many emissions, each small). A producer that would print more calls `output.Spill(root, verb, ext, bytes)`, which writes `artifacts/agents/output/<verb>-<UTC RFC3339 nanoseconds>-<pid>-<8 hex nonce>.<ext>` under the control root with O_EXCL (a collision refuses and retries with a fresh nonce) and returns the reference `{"outputMode":"file","schemaVersion":1,"verb":...,"path":...,"bytes":...,"digest":"sha256:...","format":"json|text"}`; the producer prints the reference as one line (as JSON when JSON was asked for, else the same fields in one text line).
- Compatibility: the no-path mode of `publishTestingResult` gains the envelope only when the result exceeds the bound; a caller that unmarshals a TestResult and finds `outputMode` dereferences the path (the two readers in the repository, the seat's shell and `scripts/agents/test-result-summary.sh`, are taught in the same slice; the landing path never sees the envelope). The reference's `schemaVersion` is the envelope's own, distinct from TestResult's.
- The landing script: when a step fails, land.sh copies its complete combined step log to `artifacts/agents/output/land-<UTC nanoseconds>-<pid>.log` BEFORE its EXIT cleanup runs, prints the forty-line tail as today, and prints the reference line naming the copy; nothing is deleted before the copy exists. The full coverage-delta failure text keeps printing in full (a bounded, human-read artifact) and is also in the copy.
- Retention: the output directory is run-scoped operational data (`artifacts/**`, excluded from every projection); files older than seven days are removed by the same janitor that goal run-scoped-build-caches-have-a-janitor delivers, and until it lands by `metasystem output prune --root <root> --older-than 7d`, added in this slice.
- Dereference owner: the reading side of section 3 (the seat's instruction: open the referenced path with a bounded view) and the Stop hook's turn verdict, which prints the newest reference line of the turn so the path is on the screen.
- Tests, to be added: one per producer (the no-path TestResult over the bound spills and names the file; a failed landing step's copy exists after the script's EXIT; two concurrent spills of one verb take distinct names; a file already present under the chosen name is not overwritten).

Native reads a seat makes with its own tools (a file read, a grep) are the harness's surface: bounded by the instruction of section 3 and measured by section 7, never claimed by construction.

## 3. Briefs and evidence travel by path (by construction in the packet, by instruction in the reading)

Today the composition owner embeds the whole brief and every continuation body into the delegate packet (internal/dispatch/composition.go) and records source and delivered digests. The contract of this revision:

- A typed reference: `{"path":"<control-root-relative>","digest":"sha256:...","bytes":N,"lifetime":"immutable|staged","purpose":"brief|design|critique|return|diff|log|evidence"}`. The path is relative to the control root of the chain (the installation's artifacts store or a tracked file), never absolute and never outside the root (`..` and symlinks refused as ComposeRolePacket refuses sources today).
- The packet: `references[]` carries the typed references; the directive text (the task sentence and the section list) stays inline up to `MaxInlineBytes`; a brief, a design page, a critique body, a return, a diff, a log is never copied into the packet, a notification, a follow-up or the handoff state file.
- Lifetime: `immutable` names a file under the control root that no writer changes after composition (the chain's rounds, the tracked plans and records at a commit); `staged` names a verified copy the composition owner made under `artifacts/agents/<job>/staged/` for a recipient on another host, with the same digest.
- Verification: immediately before the model invocation, the runtime adapter (the one place every rostered runtime passes through, scripts/agents/hosts/<runtime>.sh calling `job verify-references --job <id>`) re-reads every referenced file and compares digest and byte count; a mismatch or an unreadable file refuses the launch with `REFERENCE_MISMATCH path=... expected=... found=...` and the job record carries the refusal; nothing is dereferenced by prose.
- Instruction: the brief template (scripts/agents/templates/brief.md), the three role contracts (design-critic, code-critic, implementer) and docs/orchestration.md say: request the smallest section you need, read a large file through a bounded view, and open a reference only through its path.
- Tests, to be added: a composed packet with a 60 KB brief carries a reference and no body; a reference whose file changed between composition and launch refuses the launch by name; a staged copy verifies on a second host root; a path outside the control root refuses at composition.

## 4. Account for the context of each call (by measurement)

Per-call context means the whole prompt presented to one provider invocation, not a sum over invocations, and the engine reads what the harness recorded after the fact. One package owns the reading: `internal/spend` gains the typed API `LatestCall(root, seat) (CallSample, error)` and `Calls(root, seat, since) ([]CallSample, error)`, where `CallSample{Runtime, SessionID, InvocationID, PromptTokens, CacheCreation, CacheRead, At, Source}`.

- Claude: the seat's session is the announced main (artifacts/agents/mains/<session>-<pid>.json carries the sessionId); the transcript is `~/.claude/projects/<slug>/<sessionId>.jsonl`; a sample is one assistant record keyed by `requestId` (input plus cache creation plus cache read, the formula already in spend/transcript.go), deduplicated by requestId. Codex: the announced session names the rollout; a sample is one `event_msg.token_count` record's `info.last_token_usage` (input tokens, cached input a subset), keyed by the record's ordinal in the rollout because the rollout carries no request id (verified on this box on 2026-09-12). Devin and the ACP outcome report per adapter invocation and give `unknown` per call with the reason `per-invocation usage only`; they are outside the proof cohort until their adapters expose call-resolved usage.
- Cursor: `artifacts/agents/context/<seat>/cursor.json` holds, per runtime, the file identity (path, inode, size) and the byte offset last read; a read tails from the offset; a rotated or truncated file (identity or size below the offset) restarts from zero and says so in the sample's `Source`. Cost bound: one Stop-hook attempt reads at most the bytes appended since the last attempt; a first read of a whole transcript is bounded by the harness's own file size and happens once.
- Capability per runtime, declared on the adapter (`scripts/agents/hosts/<runtime>.sh` gains `context-sample: per-call|per-invocation|none`): a runtime without a stream answers `unknown` and the reason; an unreadable home or a missing transcript answers `unknown` with the path.
- `RoleContext` joins the steward's health roles (internal/steward/health.go) and calls `LatestCall`; `ContextBoundTokens = 150000` and `ContextCeilingTokens = 200000` live in internal/steward/context.go. The role renders one line, `context: N thousand tokens this call, bound 150, ceiling 200`, or `context: unknown (<reason>)`. The Stop hook prints the line on each Stop-hook attempt as one accelerator; `metasystem context status --root <installation>` prints it on any runtime with no hook.
- There is no admission in front of a provider invocation: a call above the ceiling is a recorded breach that fails the week (section 7), not a prevented call. This is the measured half of Wido's reading.
- Tests, to be added: `LatestCall` reads a synthetic Claude transcript and a synthetic Codex rollout to the exact figure; a rotated file restarts the cursor and says so; a per-invocation adapter answers `unknown` with the reason; the second read of an unchanged file reads nothing.

## 5. Hand the seat to a fresh session (by construction, on the runtimes whose main the engine can see die)

At the bound the seat hands off to itself; at the ceiling the turn verdict says so in one command. Exclusivity is Wido's decision (2): the successor launches only after the engine has observed the predecessor's death.

- `metasystem context handoff --root <installation>` writes an immutable, nonce-bound state file `artifacts/agents/context/<seat>/<nonce>/state.json` (schema 1): the held goal (id, accepted revision, claimant), its next step (text, source page path and digest), the open items (stable ids, status, evidence paths), the last landings (commit ids, goal ids, receipt paths), the scratch pointers (purpose, path, digest, required or optional) and the messages owed (recipient, subject, draft path, delivery status); every path is a typed reference of section 3 and is verified at write; the file stays under 32 KB by pointing at manifests. The verb refuses on a runtime whose main it cannot observe (`HANDOFF_UNOBSERVABLE runtime=<r>`), refuses when a required reference does not verify, and refuses when the seat holds no goal (a seat with nothing to hand off just stops).
- The verb then stages the continuation intent through the existing staging (internal/steward/stage.go StageIntent) with a new kind `seatHandoff`, carrying the nonce, the state file's digest, and the predecessor's exact process identity (pid, start time, the announced main). Then it returns; it ends no harness. The predecessor's own end is the harness's act: a headless continuation session exits at the end of its turn; an interactive session ends when the person closes it or the Stop hook allows the stop under the recorded handoff (the hook reads the intent and prints `handoff recorded: <nonce>; end this session` as the stop condition, an allowance, not a refusal).
- `decideForRevival` (internal/steward/revive.go) treats `seatHandoff` unlike `seatIdle`: it launches only when the predecessor identity in the intent is provably dead (internal/identity, the same prover the reaper uses: pid gone or start time changed); a live or unknown predecessor keeps the intent staged and the tick says so; the `seatIdle` exemption that launches beside a live main stays what it is for its own kind. The continuation brief names the nonce and the state file's digest; the continuation contract (scripts/agents/roles/steward-continuation.md) gains the rule to read the state file first, verify its digest, and hold the same goal; a state file whose digest differs from the intent's is refused and the tick names it.
- Two handoffs cannot cross: each writes its own nonce directory; a later handoff never overwrites an earlier state file; a delayed continuation of an earlier intent finds its own file. A predecessor that comes back to life after the successor launched is impossible by construction on Claude and Codex (a dead pid is dead); on a runtime without an observable main the verb refused at step one.
- Quiescence: the handoff happens at a harness turn boundary with no tool call in flight; the verb checks the seat's own records (every authorised message delivered, every required result on disk) and refuses otherwise; what the harness holds only in memory is disposable by declaration and the state file says so.
- Tests, to be added: the handoff verb refuses an in-flight required result, a mismatched reference and an unobservable runtime, and writes a valid nonce-bound state file otherwise; two handoffs in a row leave two state files; `decideForRevival` refuses a `seatHandoff` intent while the predecessor pid lives and launches it once the pid is gone; a continuation refuses a state file whose digest differs from its intent.

## 6. Runtime independence

The verbs, the spill owner, the reference verification, the usage readers, the health role, the state file and the handoff live in the Go engine and the adapter records; a Claude hook and a Codex plugin are accelerators of the same verbs. Claude and Codex prove the whole path with real coordinator work; Devin proves its reading per adapter invocation, its `unknown` per call and the handoff verb's refusal; the reference verification runs on every runtime because every launch passes the adapter.

## 7. Proof and its record

One seven-day UTC window after slice 3 lands. The cohort is the recorded samples of section 4: for Claude every assistant record with a requestId in the seat's transcripts, for Codex every token-count record in its rollouts; the report says plainly that a call the harness did not record is outside the cohort, and a runtime with no per-call stream is outside it. ## 9. Risks, self-grade, reject condition

Risks: a runtime that reports per adapter invocation, read as per call, would fabricate a figure (refused: `unknown`); path references can hide a missing file (verification before every launch and at the handoff write); a fresh session loses cache warmth and orients again (the week's report records handoffs beside the prompt sizes); the engine cannot see a call the harness does not record (the cohort is the recorded samples and the report says so); an interactive predecessor that never exits keeps its handoff staged forever (the tick says so each round and the turn verdict prints the allowance; the seat is not two coordinators, it is one that has not left). Self-grade: every mechanism is labelled by construction or by measurement per Wido's reading; the six round-2 findings each have a mechanism in sections 2 to 5 and 7; the reject condition: reject this revision if a mechanism is claimed by construction where the engine does not own the surface, if a per-invocation figure is presented as a call, if a `seatHandoff` intent can launch beside a live predecessor, if a reference can be dereferenced without verification before a launch, or if the week's pass can be met by samples the report does not cover.
