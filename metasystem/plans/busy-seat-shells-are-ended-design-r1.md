## 1. The problem

On 2026-09-14 seat m1b's Bash tool ran a descendants walk that never ends under zsh; the tool's timeout moved it to the background and it burned 65 to 70 percent of a core for twelve hours (`records/misc/leaked-processes-diagnosis-2026-09-15.md:73-80`, class C). The captured process is `/bin/zsh -c source /Users/wido/.claude/shell-snapshots/snapshot-zsh-...sh 2>/dev/null || true && ...` (`records/misc/leaked-processes-evidence-2026-09-15.txt:3`). Its loop forked `pgrep`, `tr` and `echo` continuously, so at any instant it may have a child; the CPU was nonetheless the shell's own.

Critique round 2 (`records/misc/seat-machines-shed-leaked-processes-critique-r2.md`, SMLP-204 to -206) showed that revision 2's predicate could not be built: two sightings ten minutes apart do not span 30 minutes; sampled child absence proves nothing about the interval between samples; and a notification queued on "the acting checkout" does not reach the shell's session.

## 2. What the engine can prove, and how each finding is answered

**The kernel keeps the proof for us.** Every process carries a monotone accumulator of the CPU time its own threads consumed. A child's time is charged to the child; on exit it is folded into the parent's separate children figure, which this design never reads. Two readings of the shell's own accumulator, taken any time apart, therefore give the exact CPU the shell itself burned over the whole interval, with nothing in between needed and nothing of its children included. That is the evidence the intent asks for.

- SMLP-204 (a 30 minute baseline): the registry keeps every sighting younger than twice the bound; the predicate is evaluated over the newest sighting and any retained sighting at least the bound earlier (P5). With the default ten-minute tick (`internal/steward/runner.go:97-105`) three ticks are retained before the fourth decides.
- SMLP-205 (sampled absence): "no child for 30 minutes" is not sampled at all. The proof that the CPU was not in the children is the accumulator itself (a parent blocked in `wait` on a long `go test` or `shasum` gains almost no self time). What remains is the instantaneous safety that the shell is not carrying live work when it is signalled: a child seen alive with the same exact identity ten seconds apart is a persistent child and refuses the action, rechecked immediately before TERM and again before KILL (P6). A transient fork of the bad loop cannot pass that test twice; a child that appears after the last probe is orphaned, not signalled (section 7).
- SMLP-206 (the shell's session): the owner is the announced main that is the shell's parent, read from the checkout's `artifacts/agents/mains/` records, which carry `sessionId`, `pid` and `pidStartedAt` (`internal/census/run.go:610-660`; live record `artifacts/agents/mains/19c52dfc-...-11601.json`). The owner is recorded at the first sighting and kept when the parent dies, so a reparented shell keeps its session (P3). The notification is queued on the owner checkout, whose steward delivers it (P7), and a health role puts it in the stop report every governed Stop reads (P8).
- SMLP-214 (a cgo-free reader): P2, built beside the existing `proc_info` syscall use (`internal/identity/identity_darwin.go:145-175`).

Live facts (this machine, arm64, 2026-09-16 15:27): this delegate's own tool shell is `zsh -c source ~/.claude/shell-snapshots/...` with parent 58782, `claude -p ... --name design-bss`, and that headless parent is announced (`artifacts/agents/mains/97e2ff...-58782.json`): headless delegates are mains too. `sysctl hw.tbfrequency` is 24000000, the timebase the Darwin reader divides by.

Why a tool shell alive for 30 minutes is always a candidate: the Bash tool's foreground timeout is at most ten minutes, so any tool shell older than the bound was backgrounded or abandoned. Nothing in this page depends on that; it explains why the bound is safe.

## 3. Rules

Package: new `internal/busyshell` (with `testmain_test.go`, c15d23be). Every rule names its witness and the mutation that makes the witness fail. Injected seams, as R-104-m1e requires: `Clock` (now), `Sleeper`, `Prober` (`identity.Prober`, `identity.go:171`), `CPUReader`, `Children` (pid lister plus parent reader) and `Signal`.

- **P1 (shape).** A process is a seat tool shell when argv[0]'s base is `zsh`, `bash` or `sh`, argv[1] is `-c`, and argv[2] begins with `source ` followed by a path whose parent directory is `<home>/.claude/shell-snapshots/`. An unreadable argv (`Exact.ArgvKnown` false, `identity.go:41-47`) never matches. A shell that `exec`s its last command leaves the shape at its next sighting and is dropped. Witness: `TestToolShellShape` (the evidence argv matches; a user's `zsh -c` without the prefix, a `/bin/sh -c` hook, and an argv-unknown row do not). Mutation: drop the parent-directory check and the hook row matches.
- **P2 (the CPU reader).** `identity.CPUTime(pid) (time.Duration, bool)`. Darwin: `SYS_proc_info` (336) with `PROC_INFO_CALL_PIDINFO` (2), flavor `PROC_PIDTASKINFO` (4), a 96 byte buffer, return size checked equal to 96; `pti_total_user` at offset 16 plus `pti_total_system` at offset 24, in mach absolute units, divided by `hw.tbfrequency` read once through `unix.SysctlUint64` (the package already uses `unix.SysctlRaw` and `SysctlKinfoProc`, `enumerate_darwin.go:47,116`). Linux: fields 14 and 15 of `/proc/<pid>/stat` after the last `)`, the split `parseProcStat` already uses (`identity_linux.go:115-138`), over `userHZ` (`identity_linux.go:44-48`). Elsewhere and on any error: `false`. Witnesses: `TestCPUTimeOfSelfAgreesWithRusage` (own pid against `Getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF)`, within one second); `TestCPUTimeIsMonotoneAcrossASpin` (spin until rusage shows 100 ms more, then the reader shows at least 50 ms more; bounded by CPU, never wall); `TestCPUTimeOfASleepingChildStaysNearZero` (a `sleep` child, under 100 ms after 200 ms of wall); `TestCPUTimeOfAGonePidIsUnknown`. Mutations: use the wrong timebase and the rusage witness fails by a factor of 24; read offset 8 and the spin witness fails.
- **P3 (owner).** At every sighting the pass reads the shell's parent (`identity.ParentPid`, `enumerate_darwin.go:112-129`). If an announcement in this checkout's `artifacts/agents/mains/`, or in `.claude/worktrees/*/metasystem/artifacts/agents/mains/` beneath it, has `pid` equal to the parent and `pidStartedAt` equal to the parent's start second (`KernelProber.ReadStart`, `identity_darwin.go:53`), the owner is `session:<sessionId>` with `mainId`, provenance `parent-main`, and it is written into the entry once; later sightings never overwrite it, so a shell whose main has since died keeps its session (the durable owner record SMLP-206 asks for). If no announcement matches and no entry exists: a live, unannounced parent makes the shell `foreign` (another checkout's, or a runtime that never announced) and it is not tracked; a parent of 1 or a dead parent makes it `ownerless`, tracked with provenance `ownerless`. Witness: `TestOwnerBySessionThenDurableThenOwnerless` (fixture mains directory, fake prober: parent announced gives the session; the same entry after the parent dies keeps it; a never-announced dead parent gives ownerless; a live unannounced parent gives foreign). Mutation: resolve the owner afresh each tick and the durable row loses its session.
- **P4 (the registry).** One JSON document per checkout, `artifacts/agents/steward/busy-shells.json`, written through `atomicfile.WriteText` and read, reduced and replaced inside the tick, which already holds the tick arbitration lock (`internal/steward/tick.go:104-112`); no second lock. Entry key: the full `identity.Ref` (`identity.go:25-31`), so a reused pid is a new entry. Entry fields: `ref`, `owner{sessionId,mainId,provenance}`, `argvHead` (first 200 bytes), `firstSeen`, `sightings[{at,cpu}]`, `state` (`sighted`, `kept-live-child`, `ended`, `survived`, `gone`), `lastAction`. Reduce: append this tick's sighting; drop sightings older than twice the bound but never the newest; an entry whose process reads Dead by identity becomes `gone` and is dropped on the following tick; an entry unseen for twice the bound is dropped. A reading that fails (`known=false`) appends nothing and keeps the entry. An unreadable or malformed file is replaced by an empty registry with one log line; it never fails the tick. Witnesses: `TestRegistryRetainsTheBaselineAcrossTicks` (seven ten-minute sightings on an artificial clock retain the four that span the bound), `TestRegistryRecoversFromACorruptFile`, `TestRegistryKeysOnExactIdentity`. Mutation: keep only the last two sightings and the baseline witness fails.
- **P5 (the predicate).** An entry is busy when there exist retained sightings i and n (n newest) with `at_n - at_i >= bound` and `cpu_n - cpu_i >= fraction × (at_n - at_i)`; bound default 30 minutes, fraction 25 percent. This is the goal's "burns CPU with no child for 30 minutes" made provable: the CPU counted is the shell's own by the kernel's accounting, so a shell whose work is in its children cannot satisfy it. Witness: `TestBusyPredicateOverSightings` with rows: 31 minutes at 100 percent self CPU, busy; 40 minutes at 0.5 percent self CPU beside a hot child (its CPU is not in the readings), not busy; 20 minutes at 100 percent, not busy (span short); idle 3 hours then busy 40 minutes, busy at the first retained pair that qualifies; readings that go down (a reused pid) never qualify. Mutation: compare against the oldest sighting only and the idle-then-busy row fails; drop the span check and the 20 minute row fails.
- **P6 (the child guard, beside each signal).** Immediately before TERM, and again before KILL, the pass lists the shell's live children (all pids whose parent is the shell, `identity.AllPids` plus `ParentPid`), sleeps `guard-seconds` (default 10), and lists again; a child present in both lists with the same exact identity (`identity.Compare`, `identity.go:122`) is persistent, and the action is refused this tick: state `kept-live-child`, the child's pid and argv head logged, one notification (P7). The refusal does not reset the baseline; the next tick re-evaluates. Children are never signalled. Witnesses: `TestGuardRefusesOnAPersistentChild` (a real `sleep 30` child, recorded and reaped by the test's own cleanup, refuses; a fake signal records nothing), `TestGuardIgnoresTransientChildren` (fake children lists disjoint across the two probes proceed), `TestGuardRunsBeforeKillToo` (the fake sleeper's calls are counted: two before TERM, two before KILL). Mutation: probe once and the transient witness's disjoint lists cannot be told from a persistent child.
- **P7 (the action).** For a busy entry that passed the guard: TERM to the pid alone, never to a group (the shell's group can be its main's), through `identity.SignalExact` if fixture-children's unit has landed it (its page, section 3.2) or `proofrun.SignalAuthenticated` otherwise (`internal/proofrun/watchdog.go:284-297`), which proves the exact identity beside the signal; poll `AliveRef` for `TermGrace` (5 s); if still Alive, the guard again, then KILL, then `KillGrace` (1 s). Outcome `ended`, `survived` (alive after KILL: logged as a defect, health goes dead, P8), or `gone` (identity mismatch: nothing sent). Every outcome appends a JSON line to `artifacts/agents/steward/busy-shells.log` with ref, owner, span, self CPU seconds, outcome and argv head, and queues `steward.QueueNotification(ownerCheckout, {Nonce: "busy-shell-<pid>-<startMicro>-<outcome>", Message: "the steward ended a busy seat shell of session <id>: pid N burned M CPU seconds over S minutes with no child: <argv head>"})` (`internal/steward/intervene.go:330-350`). The owner checkout is the checkout whose pass tracks the entry (P3), so delivery uses that checkout's channel (`internal/steward/notify.go:41-56`: configured command, the fixture log for fixture enrollments, or the platform notification) on the next `DeliverPending` (`notify.go:113`), and the message shows in `steward status` and `steward pending` (`cmd/metasystem/steward_verbs.go:788,815`). An ownerless shell's message says "its session is gone" and goes to the acting checkout. Witness: `TestActionSignalsByIdentityLogsAndNotifies` (fake signal, prober, clock and sleeper: the sequence is guard, TERM, poll, guard, KILL; the log line and the pending file under the owner checkout exist, not under a second checkout in the same fixture home). Mutations: signal the negative pgid and the fake signal's target assertion fails; queue on the acting root and the two-checkout assertion fails.
- **P8 (the health role).** `busy-shells` joins `healthRoleOrder` (`internal/steward/health.go:66-86`): alive when no entry is `kept-live-child` older than twice the bound and none is `survived`; dead on either, reason naming pid, session and minutes; unknown when the registry file is unreadable. The verdict is written to `health.json` (`health.go:220`), which the stop report reads (`internal/report/stoppresentation.go:271-272`), so the seat sees it at its next governed Stop. Witness: `TestBusyShellsHealthRole` (three registries, three verdicts). Mutation: drop the survived branch and a shell that outlived KILL reads alive.
- **P9 (wiring).** `RunTick` runs the pass after `ReapContinuations` (`tick.go:170`) and before the decision; a pass error is carried in `TickResult.BusyShells` and the tick log, never through `degradedTick`, because a leak census must not stop supervision. The pass enumerates the process table itself (`identity.AllPids` and `KernelProber.Probe`, as `census.EnumerateProcesses` does, `internal/census/production.go:45-76`), matches P1 first and reads parent and CPU only for matches. Witness: `TestTickRunsTheBusyShellPassAndSurvivesItsFailure` (a registry path made unwritable: the tick completes with the error in its result). Mutation: return the error and the witness sees a degraded tick.
- **P10 (knobs and the fixture scale).** `metasystem.steward.busy-shell-minutes` (git config, read like `TickSeconds`, `runner.go:97-105`; default 30; values outside 5 to 1440 are refused with a logged reason and the default is used) and `metasystem.steward.busy-shell-guard-seconds` (default 10, 1 to 120). In a fixture-enrolled checkout only (`VerifyIdentity` reporting `EnrollmentFixture`, as `notify.go:47-49` checks) the bound and the graces are multiplied by `METASYSTEM_FIXTURE_CAP_SCALE_MILLI/1000`, the scale the runner already applies (`runner.go:901-915`); the guard interval is never scaled. A production root ignores the variable. Witnesses: `TestBusyShellKnobsValidate`, `TestBusyShellScaleAppliesOnlyToFixtureRoots`. Mutation: apply the scale unconditionally and the production row shrinks its bound.

## 4. What is out of scope

- Seat instruction text (loop deadlines, reading backgrounded tasks): diagnosis item 5, not in this DONE; the seat may add one line to `docs/orchestration.md` by hand.
- Codex brokers, fixture stewards, retention: their own goals. This page ends only P1-shaped processes.
- Test-fixture reaping: owned by fixture-children-cannot-outlive-their-test (m1c). This page reuses its `testutil.Fixture(t)` and `Record` for every real child its witnesses spawn once those land; until then the witnesses use `exec.Cmd` with a KILL in `t.Cleanup`, and the builder states which was used.
- The upstream report on the Bash tool's timeout-to-background behaviour: stays with the seat.
- A machine-wide lock: unnecessary, because every checkout acts only on its own or ownerless entries (section 7 names the one benign overlap).

## 6. Proof of DONE

The DONE names three planted shells. The Go witnesses above prove each rule on artificial clocks and fake readings; the end-to-end proof runs real shells under a scaled bound, once, by the seat under the testrun lock (Wido: artificial clocks in tests, never load-fragile tests; a seat-run bed is evidence, not a suite member).

`scripts/agents/busy-shell-fixtures.sh` arms a fixture-enrolled root with `metasystem.steward.tick-seconds=5` and `METASYSTEM_FIXTURE_CAP_SCALE_MILLI=10` (bound 18 s, TermGrace 50 ms, KillGrace 10 ms), announces the bed's shell as the root's main and binds a session id to it (`metasystem lease announce` then `lease associate-session`, `cmd/metasystem/main.go:599-603`), writes a snapshot file under a fixture `HOME/.claude/shell-snapshots/`, and starts three children in the tool-shell shape, each recording its pid for the bed's cleanup:

1. the busy loop: `zsh -c 'source <snapshot> 2>/dev/null || true && end=$((SECONDS+120)); while (( SECONDS < end )); do :; done'`, bounded by its own deadline so a leak costs at most two minutes;
2. the long checksum: `zsh -c 'source ... && head -c 3000000000 /dev/zero | shasum -a 256'`;
3. the long go test: `zsh -c 'source ... && cd <metasystem> && go test -count=1 -run TestCPUTimeOfSelfAgreesWithRusage ./internal/identity'`.

Expected within the bound, which is bound plus two ticks plus the guard and graces (18 + 10 + 10 + 1 s, under 40 s from the loop's start): one `ended` line in `busy-shells.log` naming shell 1's ref and the bed's session id; the fixture notification log (`notify.go:47-49`) carries its message; shells 2 and 3 are alive at that moment or have exited by their own completion, with no `ended` or `kept-live-child` line naming them; the bed then waits for 2 and 3 to finish and asserts the registry holds no entry for them in a state other than `sighted` or `gone`. On any failure the bed prints the registry so a load-starved spin is diagnosable. A second run of the bed with shell 1 replaced by `zsh -c 'source ... && sleep 90 & while kill -0 $!; do :; done'` expects one `kept-live-child` line and no `ended`: the hot poll around a live child is kept, which is the "never ending a shell whose work is in its children" clause on a real process.

## 7. Residual risk, stated

- A child forked between the guard's second probe and the signal is orphaned, never signalled; its own work is not lost. The window is the signal call itself.
- Two checkouts can both track the same ownerless shell and both act in the same tick; the second signal finds a dead identity and records `gone`; at worst two notifications name one shell.
- A tool shell whose parent never announced is `foreign` until the parent dies; the engine does not act for a runtime it cannot attribute.
- A legitimate shell that computes in itself for 30 minutes (a shell-only text loop) is ended; the DONE clause defines it so, and the report tells the seat what was ended and why.
- Under heavy load a spin may fall below 25 percent of a core; the bed then reports the readings instead of passing. The Go witnesses never depend on load.
