# g1-s30 The Partner knows the territory

Revision 1 was on Wido's question "Is the project partner an expert on both the metasystem and also the UI? If not; what supporting system for the agent do we need to get in place to make this possible and easy to maintain/extend?" and his ruling "write the design, get it critiqued by astra and then build it". Builds on g1-s28 (the Partner reads and explains) and g1-s29 (it is told what the human sees and can look further).

## Outcome

The Partner answers questions about the interface and about the metasystem as an expert would: it knows what every section, act, lane, setting and record kind is for in this build, it knows what the project's memory holds and where, and it goes to the right source for a question instead of guessing. Every piece of that knowledge lives beside the thing it describes and is generated or listed from it, so adding a section, a term, a record or a skill teaches the Partner without anyone writing a second account.

## The principle

Knowledge is kept where it is true. The interface describes itself from its own sources; the project's memory is listed by its own resolver; the way to answer is a skill in the kit like every other. Nothing is copied into a manual for the Partner.

## Step 1

1. **The interface describes itself, with one owner per fact.** The manifest has two halves and \`GET /api/interface\` joins them at request time. The build generates the frontend's half from the sources the pages render from: the sections with their help text and what each shows, the lanes in order, the help terms, the suggested questions, and for each section and lane whether *this build* projects it (the same tables that decide what is rendered). The server composes the Go half at serve time from its owners: the acts with their general requirement (a signed-in human; a proven seat when no Partner is configured), the settings under \`ui.\` with their defaults and their effective values on this seat, the record kinds with their homes resolved for this checkout, the admitted runtimes. Three things are told apart everywhere: a section's *purpose*, its *availability in this build*, and *what the Partner itself can do* (read and explain, in this build). The generator refuses a source with no description; the bundle's staleness check already covers the frontend inputs, and the Go half is never static. The tool server exposes the joined manifest as \`interface()\`. Two contradictions found by the critique are corrected at their owners: the Partner's own help text no longer promises writing, and no description presents an unprojected section as working.
2. **A map at session start, dated and not authoritative.** With the first prompt of a session, and again after a recovery, the standing rule carries the memory index: the intent and doctrine chapters in reading order, then decisions, designs and open questions newest first, each one line: a record by its id, a bound document by \`doc:<path>\`, a question by its row id, with title, status where one is declared, and the one-line summary the Project pane already extracts; bounded at 300 lines and 12,000 characters, trimmed by whole kinds from the end with a line naming the kinds and counts left out, and a read failure stated rather than skipped. It is headed with the time it was composed and this sentence: "This index is a map from <time>; it is not evidence that something exists, is absent, or has the status shown. For any of those, use the tools." Later prompts carry the page snapshot and one line naming the index's time. The standing rule is composed every turn today; this is where the first-prompt material lives.
3. **A Partner skill in the kit, with a route into the kit itself.** \`skills/project-partner/SKILL.md\`, routed from \`wow.md\` like every skill, appended to the standing rule with the first prompt. *Where to look*, three territories kept apart: the **interface** (a term, a page, an act, a setting → the manifest); the **project's memory** (why → the intent book; a rule the code is held to → the doctrine book; a choice made → the decisions; how something will be built → the designs; open → the questions); and the **metasystem itself** (a concept → the kit's glossary; a verb → the command catalogue's own descriptions; a standing ruling → the rulings register; a workflow → the route \`wow.md\` names, read to explain and never undertaken). A tool, \`kit(topic)\`, answers from those owners: the glossary entry, the verb's description, the ruling row, the routed skill's summary. Observations of the ledger (which goals, what state) come only from the ledger tools and are never confused with explanations of its rules. *How to answer*: in plain words and the interface's vocabulary, citing the source and its tip or revision, saying what it could not see, and never claiming an act it cannot do.
4. **Two kinds of check.** Deterministic: the fake ACP server records the prompt it was sent and the tools it was offered; tests assert that the first prompt carries the index, the skill and the standing rule, that \`interface()\` and \`kit()\` are advertised, and that removing any of them fails; the \`interface()\` result says an unprojected section is not available and says the Partner cannot write. By hand, before a release: a script under the walkthrough asks a real runtime a dozen questions through the production path (a term, a lane, an act, a verb, a ruling, an unavailable section, "can you edit this") and keeps the question, the answer, the runtime and model, the sources read and the tip; a human reads it for contradictions, unsupported claims and wrong citations. It is smoke, not certification, and says so.

To add a section, a term, a setting, a record kind or a skill, a maintainer touches its owner and nothing written for the Partner: the route and its help text; the help register; the Go configuration owner; the resolver's kind and home; the skill and its \`wow.md\` route.

## Later, when it hurts

- Knowledge beyond the records: an adopted application's own domain, taught through its docs books.
- Usage and cost per turn on the page; the check run on a cadence by the steward.
- Answers that cite with links into the interface for every source, once the interface has pages for skills and settings.
